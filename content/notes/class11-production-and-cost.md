## Production and the production function

Production is the process of transforming inputs into goods and services. A production function describes the maximum output that can be produced from given quantities of inputs, with technology held constant.

In the short run, at least one factor is fixed. In the long run, all factors can be varied. This distinction helps explain why a firm’s output may respond differently to additional labour or capital over different time horizons.

## Total, average, and marginal product

**Total product (TP)** is the total output produced. **Average product (AP)** is output per unit of a variable input:

\[
AP = \frac{TP}{\text{units of variable input}}
\]

**Marginal product (MP)** is the additional output from one more unit of the variable input:

\[
MP_n = TP_n - TP_{n-1}
\]

When MP is above AP, AP rises. When MP is below AP, AP falls. MP crosses AP at AP’s maximum point.

## Law of variable proportions

As more units of a variable input are combined with fixed inputs, output usually passes through three stages. In the first stage, total product rises at an increasing rate. In the second stage, total product continues to rise but at a decreasing rate; marginal product is positive but falling. In the third stage, marginal product becomes negative and total product falls.

The second stage is generally the economically relevant range for a firm because the variable input still adds to total output, while the fixed input is being used intensively.

## Costs and decisions

Fixed cost does not change with output in the short run. Variable cost changes as output changes. Total cost is the sum of fixed and variable cost:

\[
TC = TFC + TVC
\]

Average cost is total cost per unit, while marginal cost is the extra cost of producing one more unit. A firm should compare the expected marginal benefit of an extra unit with its marginal cost before expanding production.
