## **From which language does the word 'Economics' originate?**

The word Economics originates from the Greek language, derived from the words 'oikos' (meaning house) and 'nemein' (meaning to manage), which together translate to "managing a household".

## What is the core subject matter that economics deals with?

Economics deals with the production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services in society, focusing fundamentally on how scarce resources with alternative uses are allocated to satisfy unlimited human wants.

## why is the study of economics subject-matter needed?

The study of economics is essential because it provides the principles, analytical tools, and logic needed to navigate scarcity, choice, and decision-making across personal, business, and societal levels. it helps us:

* Addressing the Fundamental Problem of Scarcity and Trade-offs.
* Answering Society's Central Economic Questions.
* Facilitating Managerial Decision-Making & Forward Planning.
* Guiding Public Policy, Macroeconomic Stability, and Welfare
* Empowering Rational Personal Choices in Daily Life.

## Who is considered the Father of Economics?

Adam Smith is considered the Father of Economics for separating economics from philosophy and establishing it as an independent academic discipline.



## Elucidate the various definitions of economics. Why is economics called a social science?

The word 'Economics' derives from two Greek words: 'Oikos' (a house) and 'Nemein' (to manage), signifying 'managing a household' with available scarce funds. In a broader perspective, economic thinkers have propounded four major definitions representing the evolution of economic thought.

### 1. Adam Smith's Wealth Definition (Classical View - 1776)

Adam Smith (1723–1790), known as the **Father of Economics**, defined economics in his seminal book *'An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations'* (1776) as the **'science of wealth creation'**. He established economics as an independent academic discipline.

**Main Features / Postulates:**

* **Centrality of Material Wealth:** Economics focuses exclusively on the production, acquisition, and distribution of physical wealth.
* **The 'Economic Man':** Assumes individuals are rational 'economic men' motivated by self-interest and monetary gain.
* **The Invisible Hand:** Proposes that private self-interest spontaneously promotes societal economic welfare through an 'invisible hand'.
* **Investigation of the causes of Wealth:** classical economists like adam smith believe that economics is the investigation of the causes of wealth, i.e., how wealth can be increased.

**Criticisms and Limitations:**

* **Excessive Materialism ('Dismal Science'):** Criticized by Carlyle, Ruskin, and Arnold as a 'dismal science' and 'science of mammonism' for overemphasizing riches.
* **Neglect of Human Welfare & Services:** Treats wealth as an end rather than a means to welfare, and ignores non-material services (doctors, teachers).
* **Ignores Scarcity and Choice:** Fails to address the fundamental economic problem of resource scarcity.
* **Unrealistic concept of Economic Man:** This definition is based on the unrealistic concept of economic man. as per the definition, an economic man s guided by self-interest alone but such a man does not exist in real life. He also has to carry out social, religious, political and other activities in his life.

Despite the criticisms, this definition helps to distinguish economics from other social sciences such as political science, ethics, etc.

### 2. Alfred Marshall's Welfare Definition (Neoclassical View - 1890)

Alfred Marshall (1842–1924), in *'Principles of Economics'* (1890), defined economics as: *'a study of mankind in the ordinary business of life; it examines that part of individual and social action which is most closely connected with the attainment and with the use of the material requisites of well-being.'*

**Main Features / Postulates:**

* **Primary Focus on Welfare:** Replaces wealth with human welfare as the ultimate end of economic enquiry.
* **Study of Ordinary Man:** That means it studies the rational man and ignores the activities of an irrational man like 'Sadhu' or 'Sanyasi' (ascetics) in the Hndu rreligion.
* **Social Science Classification:** according to this definition, economics is a social science studying human behavior in society.
* **Normative Science:** As per this definition, economics is a normative science since it studies what should be done to promote the material welfare of mankind.

**Criticisms and Limitations (Lionel Robbins' Critique):**

* **Welfare is Subjective & Non-Measurable:** Welfare is an abstract psychological concept not quantifiable in money.
* **Includes Harmful Goods:** Production of liquor or weapons harms welfare but remains an economic activity due to resource scarcity.
* **Narrow Material Focus:** Fails to account for non-material services that generate human welfare.
* **Economics is a Human Science:** According to the welfare definition, economics is not concerned with the man living outside society. But even an isolated man like Robinson Crusoe is also subject to the law of economics such as the law of diminishing marginal utility.

Whatever may be the criticisms, Marshallian concepts of utility and rationality are still relevant in economics.

### 3. Lionel Robbins' Scarcity Definition (1932)

Lionel Robbins (1898–1984), in *'An Essay on the Nature and Significance of Economic Science'* (1932), defined economics as: *'the science which studies human behavior as a relationship between ends and scarce means which have alternative uses.'*

**Main Features / Postulates:**

* **Unlimited Ends & Scarce Means:** Human wants are infinite, while resources (time, money, land, capital) are strictly limited.
* **Alternative Uses & Choice:** Scarce resources have alternative uses, making choice the core economic problem.
* **Neutrality Between Ends:** Treats economics as a positive science neutral regarding moral/ethical judgments. This definition shifts economics toward objectivity and precision.
* **Wants differ in Urgency:** Human wants differ in urgency. some are more urgent than others. a more urgent want is satisfied first, and less urgent wants are postponed for a future date. for example, medicine for a sick person is more urgent than wanting mo:mo.

**Criticisms and Limitations:**

* **Lacks Social Welfare Focus:** Divorces economics from social ethics, human welfare, and normative goals.
* **Ignores Macro Growth & Stability:** Fails to address macro phenomena like unemployment, inflation, and growth.
* **Too Abstract:** Lacks practical application for policy and planning.
* **Omits Production & Growth:** Neglects long-term development and innovation.
* **Unrealistic Assumptions:** Assumes rational choices, ignoring emotional/social behavior.

However, Robbins' definition is analytical, scientific and universally applicable. His definition. with minor changes here and there, is the basis of all other definitions of economics that have been given thereafter.

### 4. Paul Samuelson's Growth Definition (Modern Dynamic View - 1948)

Paul Samuelson (1915–2009) synthesized scarcity and dynamic economic growth, defining economics as the study of how society chooses to employ scarce productive resources with alternative uses to produce commodities over time and distribute them for present and future consumption.

**Main Features / Postulates:**

* **Dynamic Time Horizon:** Incorporates present and future resource allocation and economic growth.
* **Universal & Comprehensive:** Applies to barter and monetary economies, linking micro allocation with macro growth.
* **Includes Macro perspective:** It incorporates the study of macroeconomics by focusing on the growth aspects of an economy.
* It comprehensively considers all aspects of production, consumption, and distribution.
* **Considers Short-run and long-run time:** The definition acknowledges the dynamic changes in economic "means" and "ends" over time, specifically incorporating the time element.

**Criticisms and Limitations:**

* **Broad and Complex:** Too inclusive, may lose analytical clarity.
* **Overemphasis on Growth:** Assumes growth is always desirable, ignoring sustainability.
* **Still Assumes Rationality:**  Continues to rely on rational economic agents.
* **Measurement Difficulties:**  Hard to quantify intertemporal (future) welfare.
* **Ethical Neutrality:** Like Robbins, Samuelson avoids moral considerations, limiting its humanitarian scope.

### Summary: Definition Matrix

| **Economist**              | **Definition Type**         | **Focus**                               | **Limitation**                           |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Adam Smith** (1776)      | Wealth Science              | Wealth creation                         | Ignores human welfare ('Dismal Science') |
| **Alfred Marshall** (1890) | Welfare Science             | Material well-being                     | Welfare is subjective & non-measurable   |
| **Lionel Robbins** (1932)  | Scarcity & Choice Science   | Scarce means & alternative uses         | Ignores macro growth                     |
| **Paul Samuelson** (1948)  | Growth & Allocation Science | Dynamic resource optimization over time | Most comprehensive view                  |

## WHY IS ECONOMICS CALLED A SOCIAL SCIENCE?

Economics is classified as a Social Science because it systematically investigates the economic choices and behavior of human beings as members of society, using scientific methodology.

**Five Core Reasons for Social Science Classification:**

1. **Study of Human Behavior in Society:** Unlike physical sciences (Physics/Chemistry) studying inanimate matter in labs, economics analyzes how rational individuals, households, firms, and governments make optimal choices under resource scarcity.
2. **Systematized Body of Knowledge:** Possesses an organized structure of facts, concepts, and principles collected, classified, and analyzed through observation, deduction, and statistical testing.
3. **Formulates Economic Laws (Cause & Effect):** Establishes principles based on cause and effect. E.g., the Law of Demand states that price increases (cause) reduce quantity demanded (effect), holding *ceteris paribus*.
4. **Tendency & Statistical Hypotheses:** Economic laws are tendency statements or statistical hypotheses describing human tendencies in society, rather than rigid deterministic physical laws.
5. **Positive & Normative Dimensions + Art:** Functions as a **Positive Science** (explaining 'what is'), a **Normative Science** (evaluating 'what ought to be'), and an **Art** (applying principles to solve real-world problems like poverty and inflation).
