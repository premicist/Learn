## Say’s law

Say’s law is commonly expressed as the idea that production generates income sufficient to purchase output, so a general glut should not persist when markets adjust properly.

## Classical implication

If saving is channelled into investment through interest-rate adjustment, the classical model expects aggregate demand to support full-employment output.

## Criticism

Critics emphasise insufficient demand, liquidity preference, uncertainty, involuntary unemployment, rigid wages, and the possibility that planned saving does not automatically become planned investment.
