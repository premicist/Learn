## 1. What is Elasticity of Demand?

The **Law of Demand** tells us the *direction* of change: when price rises, quantity demanded falls. However, it does not tell us *by how much* demand will change.

**Elasticity of Demand** measures the **magnitude or responsiveness** of consumer demand when there is a change in price, income, or the price of related goods.

> **Definition:** Elasticity of demand is a quantitative measure of the percentage change in quantity demanded resulting from a percentage change in one of the variables affecting demand.

```
                  % Change in Quantity Demanded
  Elasticity = ───────────────────────────────────────────
               % Change in Determinant (Price, Income, etc.)
```

---

## 2. Elasticity vs. Slope of Demand Curve

It is important not to confuse the **slope** of the demand curve with its **elasticity**:

| Basis | Slope of Demand Curve | Elasticity of Demand |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Absolute change in price divided by absolute change in quantity ($\Delta P / \Delta Q$). | Relative or percentage responsiveness of quantity to price changes. |
| **Units of Measurement** | Measured in absolute physical and currency units (e.g., Rs. per kg). | Pure, unit-free percentage number or coefficient. |
| **Linear Curve Value** | Constant at every point along a straight-line demand curve. | Changes continuously at every point along a straight-line curve (from $\infty$ to $0$). |

## 3. The Three Major Types of Elasticity of Demand

Economists categorize elasticity of demand into three primary types based on which determinant is changing:

![meaning and types of elasticity of demand diagram 1](/flowcharts/introduction-to-economics/meaning-and-types-of-elasticity-of-demand-diagram-1.svg)

### 1. Price Elasticity of Demand ($E_p$)
Measures the percentage change in the quantity demanded of a commodity resulting from a percentage change in its **own price**:

$$E_p = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Demanded}}{\% \text{ Change in Price}} = -\left(\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}\right)$$

### 2. Income Elasticity of Demand ($E_y$)
Measures the percentage change in the quantity demanded resulting from a percentage change in **consumer income**:

$$E_y = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Demanded}}{\% \text{ Change in Consumer Income}} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta Y} \times \frac{Y}{Q}$$

### 3. Cross Elasticity of Demand ($E_{xy}$)
Measures the responsiveness of the quantity demanded of Good $X$ to a change in the price of a **related Good $Y$** (substitute or complement):

$$E_{xy} = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Demanded of Good X}}{\% \text{ Change in Price of Good Y}} = \frac{\Delta Q_x}{\Delta P_y} \times \frac{P_y}{Q_x}$$

---

## 4. Summary Matrix of Elasticity Types

| Elasticity Type | Triggering Factor | Standard Notation | Key Business Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Price Elasticity** | Change in product's own price ($P_x$) | $E_p$ | Setting product prices and forecasting total revenue. |
| **Income Elasticity** | Change in buyer income ($Y$) | $E_y$ | Classifying goods as luxury, normal, or inferior. |
| **Cross Elasticity** | Change in related good price ($P_y$) | $E_{xy}$ | Analyzing competitor prices and complementary products. |

## 5. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **What does elasticity of demand measure?**  
   *Answer:* The percentage responsiveness of quantity demanded to a percentage change in one of its determinants (price, income, or related prices).
2. **What does elasticity NOT measure?**  
   *Answer:* It does not measure absolute market volume or utility level; it only measures relative percentage sensitivity.
3. **Name the three primary types of elasticity of demand.**  
   *Answer:* Price elasticity ($E_p$), Income elasticity ($E_y$), and Cross elasticity ($E_{xy}$).

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Distinguish between the slope of a demand curve and the price elasticity of demand.
2. Define Price, Income, and Cross elasticity of demand with their algebraic formulas.
