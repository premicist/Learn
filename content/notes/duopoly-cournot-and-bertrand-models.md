## 1. What is a Duopoly?

A **Duopoly** is a specialized market structure in which there are **exactly two sellers** competing against each other. It represents the simplest form of an oligopoly and serves as the theoretical bridge between monopoly and oligopoly.

## 2. Augustin Cournot Model (1838) — Output Competition

Developed by French mathematician **Antoine Augustin Cournot**, this model analyzes two firms producing homogeneous goods (e.g., mineral water from natural springs) where each firm chooses its **output level** assuming the rival's output remains constant.

### Key Assumptions:
1. Two independent firms (Firm 1 and Firm 2) producing identical homogeneous goods.
2. Both firms operate with identical and constant costs (originally assumed zero marginal cost, $MC = 0$).
3. Each firm chooses its profit-maximizing output assuming the other firm's output will **not change** (Zero Conjectural Variation).

### Reaction Functions and Equilibrium:
* Each firm constructs a **Reaction Function** expressing its optimal output as a function of the rival's output:
  $$Q_1 = f(Q_2) \quad \text{and} \quad Q_2 = f(Q_1)$$
* The intersection of the two reaction curves gives the stable **Cournot Nash Equilibrium**.

### Cournot Equilibrium Outcome:
* Each duopolist produces exactly **one-third ($\frac{1}{3}$)** of the total market capacity.
* Total industry output equals **two-thirds ($\frac{2}{3}$)** of competitive market output:
  $$Q_{\text{Cournot}} = \dfrac{2}{3} Q_{\text{Competitive}}$$
* Market price is higher than competitive price but lower than pure monopoly price. Both firms earn **shared supernormal profits**.

## 3. Joseph Bertrand Model (1883) — Price Competition

French mathematician **Joseph Bertrand** criticized Cournot, arguing that in real-world business, firms compete by setting **prices**, not quantities.

### Key Assumptions:
1. Two firms sell identical homogeneous goods.
2. Both firms have identical marginal cost ($MC$).
3. Consumers buy exclusively from the firm that charges the **lower price**. If both charge the same price, the market is split 50-50.
4. Each firm sets its price assuming the competitor's price is fixed.

### The Price Undercutting Dynamic & The "Bertrand Paradox":
1. If Firm 1 sets a price above marginal cost, Firm 2 has an incentive to undercut Firm 1 by a few rupees to capture 100% of the market.
2. Firm 1 retaliates by undercutting Firm 2.
3. This mutual price war continues until neither firm can cut prices any further without incurring losses.
4. **The Bertrand Paradox:** The price war stops only when price reaches marginal cost:
   $$\mathbf{P_1 = P_2 = MC}$$
   Even though there are **only two firms** in the entire market, the market achieves the **perfectly competitive equilibrium outcome with zero economic profit**.

## 4. Comprehensive Comparison: Cournot vs. Bertrand Models

| Dimension | Augustin Cournot Model (1838) | Joseph Bertrand Model (1883) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strategic Decision Variable** | **Output / Quantity ($Q$)** | **Price ($P$)** |
| **Core Assumption** | Assumes rival's output remains constant | Assumes rival's price remains constant |
| **Market Reaction** | Smooth adjustment via Reaction Functions | Aggressive price undercutting |
| **Equilibrium Price** | $P_{\text{Cournot}} > MC$ (Above competitive price) | $P_{\text{Bertrand}} = MC$ (Competitive price) |
| **Equilibrium Industry Output** | $Q = \dfrac{2}{3} Q_{\text{Competitive}}$ | $Q = Q_{\text{Competitive}}$ (Full market volume) |
| **Firm Profitability** | Shared **Supernormal Economic Profits** | **Zero Economic Profit** (Normal profit only) |
| **Real-World Relevance** | Capital-intensive industries with fixed capacities (automobiles, aircraft) | Retail price-matching and digital service markets |

## 5. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **Define a Duopoly market.**  
   *Answer:* A market structure with exactly two sellers competing against each other.
2. **What is the decision variable in Cournot's model versus Bertrand's model?**  
   *Answer:* In Cournot, firms choose output quantities; in Bertrand, firms choose prices.
3. **What is the Bertrand Paradox?**  
   *Answer:* The phenomenon where price competition between only two firms drives market price down to marginal cost ($P = MC$), yielding the perfectly competitive outcome.

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Explain the Cournot duopoly model and how reaction functions determine equilibrium output.
2. Compare and contrast the Cournot and Bertrand duopoly models with a summary table.
