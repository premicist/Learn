## Cost-plus pricing

Cost-plus pricing sets a price by adding a margin to an estimated unit cost. It is simple and can support cost recovery when demand is relatively predictable.

## Mark-up

A mark-up is the amount added above a cost base. The percentage can be calculated on cost or on selling price, so the base must be stated clearly.

## Contribution and capacity

For short-run decisions, contribution toward fixed cost may matter more than fully allocated average cost when spare capacity exists. Long-run prices must still cover economic cost.

## Limits

Cost-plus pricing may ignore demand, competition, customer value, capacity constraints, and strategic response. A manager should compare the calculated price with market evidence.

## Quick check

Explain why a 20 percent mark-up on cost is not the same as a 20 percent margin on selling price.
