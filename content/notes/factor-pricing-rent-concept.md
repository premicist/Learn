## Define rent in economics.

Rent is the payment received by the owner for the use of a factor of production. The classical view states that rent is the price paid for the use of land to its owner, whereas the modern view states that rent is the payment made for any factor of production whose supply is perfectly inelastic(i.e., it's fixed in supply).

## What is contract rent?

Contract rent is the rent paid by a tenant to a landlord for the use of land, as determined by the contract between them. It may differ according to the quality of the land.

## Define economic rent.

Economic rent is the excess payment made to any factor of production whose supply is perfectly inelastic. It may differ according to the productivity of the factor of production.

## Difference Between Contract Rent and Economic Rent.

| Basis of Difference            | Contract Rent                                                                                                                         | Economic Rent                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Meaning**                    | The total amount of rent that a tenant agrees to pay to the landowner.                                                                | The extra income earned from land or another factor of production.                                                         |
| **Nature**                     | It is called **gross rent** because it includes rent and other payments, such as interest on improvements.                            | It is called **pure rent** because it refers only to the surplus payment for using land or a factor.                       |
| **Constituents**               | It may include economic rent, interest on money invested in land, buildings, fences, drainage, parking space, and other improvements. | It includes only the payment for the use of land or a factor of production. It does not include interest or other charges. |
| **Source**                     | It can be received from all types or grades of land.                                                                                  | It is not received from marginal land.                                                                                     |
| **Effect of Product Price**    | A change in the price of the product usually does not affect contract rent.                                                           | A change in the price of the product can affect economic rent.                                                             |
| **Included in Price and Cost** | It is included in the cost of production and therefore affects the price of a product.                                                | It is not included in the cost of production, so it does not directly affect the price of a product.                       |
| **Determination**              | It is determined by the agreement between the landowner and tenant, mainly based on demand and supply of land.                        | It is determined by the extra productivity or earning capacity of the land.                                                |

## Critically explain the Ricardian Theory of Rent with a diagram.

“Rent is the price paid for the original and indestructible power of soil.”


The Ricardian Theory of Rent was propounded by the classical economist David Ricardo. According to Ricardo, “Rent is that portion of the produce of the earth which is paid to the landlord for the original and indestructible powers of the soil.” Rent arises due to differences in the fertility of the soil.


**Assumptions:**

The Ricardian explanation of origin and determination of rent is based on the following assumptions.


* Land is a free gift of nature and its supply is fixed.
* Land is graded according to fertility.
* Land is only used for farming.
* Cultivation over new land starts from most fertile to less fertile land.
* Marginal land plays vital role to determine the rent.
* There exists perfect competition. 
* There exist a closed economy, and related classical conditions.

Two Situations:

**A. Rent under extensive use of the land**

It refers to expanding production by using more plots of land. If food demand rises, farmers first use the best land. When the existing land cannot supply enough, lower-quality land are brought into cultivation. Rent is the difference between the output of a better land and the output of the marginal land, given the same cost conditions.

The Ricardian Theory of Rent can be explained with the following schedule:

![](/images/uploads/ricardian_theory_of_rent.png "Table: Determination of Rent (Output in Kg)")

There are A, B, C, and D four grades of land according to their fertility where A is most superior and B, C, and D represents second, third and last grade of lands.\
Suppose, when people enter into the inland, they start to cultivate A grade land. but as population raises, more demand for food increases then people start cultivating B, C and D grade lands because their supply is fixed. Rent arises from differences infertility: 60 kg, 40 kg, 20 kg, and zero respectively. 

Here, Grade D is marginal land or no-rent land. it is the base of rent for other grades of land. 

**Differential rent = output of an intramarginal land − output of the marginal land ,** assuming a common cost benchmark.

![](/images/uploads/ricardian_theory_of_rent_figure.png "Figure: Ricardian Theory of Rent")

Diagram description: 

Under extensive cultivation, one unit of capital and labour employed on A, B, C, and D earns 60 kg, 40 kg, 20 kg, and 0 kg of rent respectively. The vertical shaded rectangles represent rent paid by each grade. Grade D pays no rent and is called marginal land.

**B. Rent under Intensive use of land**

The intensive use of land refers to expanding production by using more labour and capital on the same land. 

The land area remains fixed, but inputs increase. Because land is fixed, the law of diminishing returns operates where total output may rise, but each additional dose contributes less than the previous dose. Here, Rent is the difference between the output of earlier intramarginal doses (1st, 2nd and 3rd) and the marginal dose (4th).

Criticisms:

1. *Indestructible power of the soil:* Fertility decreases over time through continuous cultivation and can increase through the use of fertiliser.
2. *Wrong order of land cultivation:* Land is cultivated according to availability and accessibility, not necessarily according to fertility.
3. *The concept of rent is narrow and limited:* Rent arises not only from land but from all factors that are limited and inelastic in supply.
4. *Ignores different uses of land:* Land can be used for houses, industries, forests, and other purposes as well as cultivation.
5. *No existence of marginal land:* In reality, even inferior land may pay some rent, however small.

Despite these criticisms, the Ricardian Theory of Rent remains one of the most accepted and important theories of rent in economics.
