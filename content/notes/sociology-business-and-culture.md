## Business and cultural transformation

Businesses operate within changing cultural environments. Shifts in values, family patterns, technology, media, consumption, and identity can change what customers expect and how employees understand work.

Adaptation should be based on listening and evidence rather than assumptions. An organisation may adjust products, communication, workplace policies, or negotiation practices while keeping its core purpose clear.

## Enculturation and acculturation

Enculturation is the process through which people learn the culture of their own group. Acculturation describes cultural change that occurs through sustained contact between cultural groups. Both processes influence language, habits, expectations, and identity.

## Assimilation and adaptation

Assimilation occurs when a group adopts many patterns of another culture, sometimes reducing the visibility of its original practices. Adaptation is a broader process of adjusting behaviour or institutions to new social conditions. These processes can be voluntary, pressured, partial, or contested.

## Cultural differences in business

Cultural differences can affect communication styles, attitudes toward hierarchy, time, risk, teamwork, trust, and disagreement. These are tendencies for analysis, not fixed labels for every individual. Good management combines cultural awareness with direct communication and respect for personal difference.

## Marketing, HR, and negotiation

Marketing messages should fit local meanings and avoid stereotypes. Human-resource practices should support fair participation, inclusion, and clear expectations. Negotiators should clarify decision-making authority, relationship-building, timing, and acceptable forms of disagreement.

## Quick check

Imagine a team working across two cultural settings. Identify one possible communication difference and propose a management practice that makes expectations explicit without stereotyping either group.
