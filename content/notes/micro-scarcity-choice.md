## Scarcity and alternatives

Scarcity means resources are limited relative to the uses people want them to serve. Because every choice uses time, money, labour, land, or capital, alternatives must be considered.

## Opportunity cost

The opportunity cost of a choice is the value of the next-best alternative forgone. It is not the value of every alternative and is not always measured by a money payment.

## Choice and incentives

People respond to incentives, compare expected benefits and costs, and may change behaviour when prices, information, rules, or expectations change. Behaviour is not always perfectly rational, so institutions and habits matter too.

## Efficiency and trade-offs

An allocation is efficient when no available rearrangement can improve one relevant outcome without worsening another. Efficiency and equity are different questions and may require separate evaluation.
