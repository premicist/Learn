## Growth and development are related but different

**Economic growth** is an increase in real output or real income over time. **Economic development** is a broader improvement in people’s capabilities and living conditions. Development can include better health, education, employment, equality, security, and environmental quality.

Growth can support development by creating income and public resources, but growth alone does not guarantee that benefits are widely shared.

## Indicators

Common indicators include real income per person, literacy, life expectancy, school participation, employment, poverty, and measures of inequality. Each indicator answers a different question. A careful evaluation combines several indicators rather than relying on one number.

An average income can rise while some households remain unable to access health care or quality education. Distribution therefore matters.

## Capital formation and productivity

Capital formation is the process of increasing the stock of productive assets. It includes investment in machinery, infrastructure, education, skills, and systems that improve productivity. Human resources refer to people’s knowledge, health, skills, and ability to contribute to economic activity.

Capital formation supports growth, while investment in human resources can improve both productivity and the quality of life.

## Poverty, inequality, and employment

Poverty concerns insufficient resources for basic needs. Inequality concerns how income, wealth, or opportunities are distributed. Unemployment means that people willing and able to work cannot find suitable work. These problems can reinforce one another, but they are not identical and need not have identical solutions.

Policies may include productive employment, skills development, social protection, better infrastructure, access to finance, and fair opportunities. The appropriate mix depends on the causes and the group affected.
