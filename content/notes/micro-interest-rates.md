## Interest as a factor reward

Interest is the return for lending or using financial capital over time. It compensates for waiting and may also reflect risk, inflation, liquidity, and administration.

## Nominal and real interest

The nominal rate is the stated money rate. The real rate adjusts approximately for changes in purchasing power, so expected inflation matters when comparing returns.

## Interest-rate differentials

Rates differ because borrowers and investments differ in default risk, maturity, collateral, liquidity, market access, and information. A riskier or less liquid loan may require a higher return.

## Business decisions

Interest rates affect investment, working capital, debt service, saving, and valuation. A business should compare expected return with financing cost and risk.
