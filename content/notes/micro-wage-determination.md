## Labour demand and supply

Labour demand is derived from the value of the output workers help produce. Labour supply reflects wages, working conditions, preferences, skills, household needs, and alternative opportunities.

## Union models

Unions may bargain over wages and conditions through collective negotiation, restrict labour supply through membership rules, or pursue broader worker representation. Outcomes depend on bargaining power and market conditions.

## Minimum wage controversy

A minimum wage can raise earnings for covered workers but may affect employment, hours, prices, compliance, and informal work. Effects depend on the level and the labour market.

## Pay and wage differentials

Pay for performance links rewards to measured output or results. Wage differentials can reflect skills, training, risk, discrimination, location, scarcity, bargaining, and job amenities.
