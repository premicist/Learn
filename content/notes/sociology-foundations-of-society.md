## Society and community

Society is a network of social relationships that shares patterns of interaction, institutions, and cultural expectations. A community is a group connected by place, identity, interest, or shared belonging. Rural and urban communities may differ in scale, density, mobility, occupations, and interaction, but neither is socially uniform.

## Culture

Culture includes learned values, beliefs, language, symbols, knowledge, practices, and material objects. It gives people ways to interpret the world and coordinate behaviour. Culture is transmitted and changes through interaction, technology, migration, and social change.

## Socialisation

Socialisation is the process through which people learn language, norms, values, roles, and expected behaviour. Family, education, peers, media, religion, and workplaces can act as agents of socialisation. Socialisation continues throughout life as people enter new roles and institutions.

## Social institutions

Institutions are stable patterns of rules and relationships that meet important social needs. Family, marriage, education, religion, economic systems, and politics organise different parts of social life. Institutions can provide support and order while also reflecting unequal power or changing expectations.

## Norms, values, status, and roles

Values are broad ideas about what is desirable, while norms are expectations about conduct. Status is a recognised social position, and a role is the behaviour expected from that position. Role conflict occurs when expectations from different roles clash; role strain occurs when demands within one role are difficult to meet.
