## 1. What are Consumption Curves?

In ordinal utility analysis, economists use two important curves to trace how a consumer's optimal equilibrium basket changes when economic conditions vary:

1. **Income Consumption Curve (ICC):** Traces equilibrium points as **consumer income ($M$)** changes.
2. **Price Consumption Curve (PCC):** Traces equilibrium points as the **price of a good ($P_x$)** changes.

![Income Consumption Curve and Price Consumption Curve](/images/uploads/icc-and-pcc-curves.svg)

---

## 2. The Income Consumption Curve (ICC)

### Definition
The **Income Consumption Curve (ICC)** is the geometric locus of all consumer equilibrium points ($E_1, E_2, E_3$) obtained when the consumer's **money income continuously changes** while the market prices of Good X and Good Y remain strictly constant.

### Shapes of the ICC Across Commodity Types:
* **For Normal Goods:** As income rises, the budget line shifts parallel outward, and the consumer purchases more of both Good X and Good Y. The **ICC slopes upward from left to right**.
* **When Good X is an Inferior Good:** As income rises, the consumer buys less of Good X and more of Good Y. The **ICC bends backward toward the vertical $Y$-axis**.
* **When Good Y is an Inferior Good:** As income rises, the consumer buys more of Good X and less of Good Y. The **ICC bends downward toward the horizontal $X$-axis**.

> **Connection to the Engel Curve:** The Engel Curve (derived directly from the ICC) plots the relationship between consumer income on the vertical axis and the equilibrium quantity demanded of a good on the horizontal axis.

---

## 3. The Price Consumption Curve (PCC)

### Definition
The **Price Consumption Curve (PCC)** is the geometric locus of consumer equilibrium points ($E_1, E_2, E_3$) traced when the **price of Good X continuously changes**, holding consumer income ($M$) and the price of Good Y ($P_y$) constant.

As the price of Good X falls ($P_x \downarrow$), the budget line rotates outward along the $X$-axis ($AB \to AB_1 \to AB_2$). Connecting the successive tangency points with indifference curves yields the **PCC**.

---

## 4. Derivation of the Demand Curve from the PCC

One of the greatest achievements of the Indifference Curve approach is deriving the **Law of Demand** without relying on Marshall's cardinal utils:

```
[ Step 1: Price of X Falls (Px ↓) ] ──> Budget line rotates outward (AB → AB1 → AB2)
                ↓
[ Step 2: New Tangency Points ]     ──> PCC connects optimal bundles (E1, E2, E3)
                ↓
[ Step 3: Quantity Expands ]        ──> Equilibrium quantity of X grows (X1 → X2 → X3)
                ↓
[ Step 4: Plot (Px vs. Qx) ]        ──> Yields Downward-Sloping Individual Demand Curve
```

1. In the upper panel (PCC), as price falls, the consumer chooses points $E_1(X_1), E_2(X_2), E_3(X_3)$.
2. Projecting these quantities down onto a price-quantity axis ($P_x$ on the vertical axis, $Q_x$ on the horizontal axis) demonstrates that as price falls from $P_1 \to P_2 \to P_3$, quantity demanded expands from $X_1 \to X_2 \to X_3$.
3. Connecting these price-quantity coordinates produces the standard **downward-sloping Demand Curve ($DD$)**.

---

## 5. Summary Comparison: ICC vs. PCC

| Feature | Income Consumption Curve (ICC) | Price Consumption Curve (PCC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Variable Factor** | Consumer Money Income ($M$) | Price of one commodity ($P_x$) |
| **Constant Factors** | Commodity prices ($P_x, P_y$) are constant | Income ($M$) and other price ($P_y$) are constant |
| **Budget Line Behavior** | Shifts **parallel** outward or inward | **Rotates / Pivots** on one axis |
| **Normal Goods Slope** | Slopes upward to the right | Slopes downward or flattens |
| **Derived Curve** | Derives the **Engel Curve** | Derives the **Individual Demand Curve** |

## 6. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **What does the Income Consumption Curve (ICC) trace?**  
   *Answer:* The locus of consumer equilibrium points as income changes with prices constant.
2. **What curve is derived directly from the Price Consumption Curve (PCC)?**  
   *Answer:* The individual downward-sloping Demand Curve.
3. **What is the slope of the ICC when both goods are normal?**  
   *Answer:* Upward-sloping from left to right.

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Distinguish between the Income Consumption Curve (ICC) and Price Consumption Curve (PCC) with diagrams.
2. Explain how an individual demand curve is derived from the Price Consumption Curve.
