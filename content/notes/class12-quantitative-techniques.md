## Why quantitative techniques matter

Economics uses data to compare situations, identify patterns, and evaluate change. A correct calculation is only the first step; the final answer should name the measure, show the units, and explain what it means.

## Organising data

Begin by identifying the variable, the observation unit, and the time period. Arrange data in a table before calculating. Check whether a series is discrete, continuous, grouped, or based on weighted observations.

## Dispersion

Measures of dispersion describe how far observations vary around a central value. Range is the difference between the largest and smallest values. Variance and standard deviation use the squared deviations from the mean and are especially useful for comparing consistency.

For a simple population, the standard deviation is based on the square root of the average squared deviation from the mean. Keep the calculation steps visible and do not round too early.

## Index numbers

An index number compares a value in one period with a base period. The base is often set to 100. A price index above 100 indicates an increase relative to the base, while an index below 100 indicates a decrease.

## Interpreting results

State the direction, size, comparison base, and limitation of the result. A percentage change and an index number may describe the same movement in different forms, but neither automatically explains its cause.

## Quick check

For the values 4, 6, 8, and 10, calculate the mean and range. Then explain what a larger standard deviation would indicate if a second series had the same mean.
