## Fixed and variable cost

Fixed cost does not change with output in the short run, while variable cost changes as production changes. Total cost is the sum of the two:

**TC = TFC + TVC**

A firm should identify the time period before classifying a cost, because a cost that is fixed in the short run may be variable in the long run.

## Average costs

Average fixed cost is fixed cost per unit. Average variable cost is variable cost per unit. Average total cost is total cost per unit:

**ATC = TC ÷ Q**

As output rises, average fixed cost normally falls because the same fixed cost is spread over more units.

## Marginal cost

Marginal cost is the addition to total cost from producing one more unit:

**MC = Change in TC ÷ Change in Q**

Because fixed cost does not change with output, marginal cost is also the change in variable cost divided by the change in output.

## Curve relationships

The marginal-cost curve cuts the average-variable-cost and average-total-cost curves at their minimum points. When marginal cost is below an average cost, the average falls; when it is above the average, the average rises.

## Long-run thinking

In the long run, all inputs can be changed. A firm can adjust plant size, technology, and organisation. Long-run cost analysis therefore focuses on the scale at which production can be carried out efficiently.
