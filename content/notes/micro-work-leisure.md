## The labour-leisure choice

People allocate limited time between paid work and leisure. Work generates income that can purchase goods, while leisure provides direct satisfaction and recovery.

## Budget and time constraint

The wage rate is the opportunity cost of an hour of leisure. A higher wage changes the slope of the time-income constraint and can influence the preferred combination of work and leisure.

## Income and substitution effects

A higher wage makes leisure more expensive relative to consumption, encouraging work through the substitution effect. It also raises potential income, which may encourage more leisure through the income effect.

## Labour supply interpretation

The net response depends on preferences, household responsibilities, taxes, benefits, working conditions, and the range of hours available.
