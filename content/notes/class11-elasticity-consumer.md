## What elasticity measures

Elasticity measures how strongly one variable responds to a change in another. Price elasticity of demand is commonly written as:

\[
E_d = \frac{\text{percentage change in quantity demanded}}{\text{percentage change in price}}
\]

The sign is usually negative because price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions. In classification, economists often focus on the absolute value: demand is **elastic** when it is greater than one, **inelastic** when it is less than one, and **unit elastic** when it equals one.

## What affects demand elasticity

Demand tends to be more elastic when close substitutes are available, when the good takes a large share of income, and when consumers have more time to adjust. Necessities with few substitutes often have relatively inelastic demand.

Elasticity is not the same as the slope of a curve. Slope uses changes in levels, while elasticity uses percentage changes.

## Utility and the consumer

Utility is the want-satisfying power of a good or service. **Total utility** is the total satisfaction from all units consumed. **Marginal utility** is the additional satisfaction from one more unit:

\[
MU_n = TU_n - TU_{n-1}
\]

The law of diminishing marginal utility states that, after some point, marginal utility falls as consumption of successive units rises, other things remaining constant.

## Equi-marginal choice

A consumer who allocates a limited budget across goods aims to obtain the greatest possible satisfaction. In the basic cardinal approach, the condition is expressed as equal marginal utility per unit of money across the goods purchased:

\[
\frac{MU_x}{P_x} = \frac{MU_y}{P_y}
\]

The condition is a guide to choice, not a claim that real decisions can always be measured perfectly.

## Consumer surplus

Consumer surplus is the difference between what a consumer is willing to pay and what the consumer actually pays. On a demand diagram, it is represented by the area below the demand curve and above the market price, up to the quantity purchased.

## Practice prompt

If the price of a good falls by 10 percent and quantity demanded rises by 20 percent, classify demand using the absolute value of the elasticity. Then explain why the same good might be more elastic over a longer time period.
