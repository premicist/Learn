## 1. Introduction: Multi-Commodity Consumer Choice

While the Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility explains consumer choice for a single good, in real life consumers must allocate a limited money budget across **multiple commodities** (e.g., food, housing, transport, entertainment).

The **Law of Equi-Marginal Utility** (also called **Gossen's Second Law**, the **Law of Substitution**, or the **Principle of Maximum Satisfaction**) explains how a rational consumer maximizes total utility when choosing among various goods.

## 2. Statement & Mathematical Equilibrium Condition

> **Statement of the Law:** A rational consumer maximizes total satisfaction by allocating their limited income across various goods in such a way that the **Marginal Utility per Rupee spent** is equal for all goods.

### The Equilibrium Equation:

$$\dfrac{MU_x}{P_x} = \dfrac{MU_y}{P_y} = \dots = \dfrac{MU_n}{P_n} = MU_m$$

Subject to the consumer's total budget constraint:

$$P_x \cdot X + P_y \cdot Y = M$$

* $MU_x, MU_y$ = Marginal utility of Goods $X$ and $Y$
* $P_x, P_y$ = Market prices of Goods $X$ and $Y$
* $MU_m$ = Marginal utility of money
* $M$ = Total consumer income or budget

---

## 3. Tabular Schedule Demonstration

Suppose a consumer has a total budget of **Rs. 24** to spend on two goods: Good $X$ (Price = Rs. 2 per unit) and Good $Y$ (Price = Rs. 3 per unit).

| Units Purchased | $MU_x$ (Utils) | $\dfrac{MU_x}{P_x} \ (P_x = 2)$ | $MU_y$ (Utils) | $\dfrac{MU_y}{P_y} \ (P_y = 3)$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1st Unit** | 20 | **10 Utils/Rs.** | 36 | **12 Utils/Rs.** |
| **2nd Unit** | 16 | **8 Utils/Rs.** | 30 | **10 Utils/Rs.** |
| **3rd Unit** | 12 | **6 Utils/Rs.** | 24 | **8 Utils/Rs.** |
| **4th Unit** | 8 | **4 Utils/Rs.** | 18 | **6 Utils/Rs.** |
| **5th Unit** | 4 | **2 Utils/Rs.** | 12 | **4 Utils/Rs.** |
| **6th Unit** | 0 | **0 Utils/Rs.** | 6 | **2 Utils/Rs.** |

### Finding the Equilibrium Basket:
The consumer achieves equilibrium by equalizing marginal utility per rupee:
* $\dfrac{MU_x}{P_x} = \dfrac{MU_y}{P_y} = 6\text{ Utils per Rupee}$
* The consumer buys **3 units of Good X** and **4 units of Good Y**.
* **Budget Verification:** $(3 \times \text{Rs. } 2) + (4 \times \text{Rs. } 3) = 6 + 18 = \text{Rs. } 24$.
* **Total Utility Maximized:** $TU = (20 + 16 + 12) + (36 + 30 + 24 + 18) = 48 + 108 = \mathbf{156\text{ Utils}}$.  
  *(Any other combination of Goods X and Y will yield less than 156 utils).*

## 5. Practical Applications of the Law

1. **Consumer Consumption:** Guides households to maximize happiness from their monthly income.
2. **Production Management (Least-Cost Combination):** Producers allocate capital and labor such that marginal physical products per rupee are equalized:
   $$\dfrac{MP_L}{P_L} = \dfrac{MP_K}{P_K}$$
3. **Public Finance and Government Budgeting:** Governments allocate tax revenues across public health, national defense, and education to maximize overall social welfare.
4. **Income Distribution:** Guides the distribution of national income across rent, wages, interest, and profit.

---

## 6. Limitations of the Law

* **Indivisible Goods:** Expensive bulky goods (cars, refrigerators) cannot be purchased in small fractional units.
* **Consumer Ignorance and Habits:** Consumers often buy out of habit, custom, or impulse rather than calculating exact marginal utility ratios.
* **Utility is Subjective:** In reality, measuring exact utility in numerical units is difficult.

## 7. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **What is Gossen's Second Law?**  
   *Answer:* The Law of Equi-Marginal Utility (or Law of Substitution).
2. **State the mathematical condition for consumer equilibrium in a two-commodity world.**  
   *Answer:* $\dfrac{MU_x}{P_x} = \dfrac{MU_y}{P_y} = MU_m$.
3. **What is the alternative name for the Law of Equi-Marginal Utility?**  
   *Answer:* The Principle of Maximum Satisfaction.

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Explain the Law of Equi-Marginal Utility with a numerical schedule and diagram.
2. Discuss the application of the Equi-Marginal principle in production and government budgeting.
