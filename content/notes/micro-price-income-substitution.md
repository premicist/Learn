## Price effect

The price effect is the total change in quantity demanded caused by a change in the price of a good, holding other relevant conditions in view. It combines income and substitution effects.

## Substitution effect

When a good becomes relatively cheaper, consumers tend to substitute toward it and away from alternatives. This effect works in the direction of the relative price change.

## Income effect

A price change changes real purchasing power. For a normal good, increased real income tends to increase demand; for an inferior good, it may reduce demand.

## Normal and inferior goods

The combined effect generally produces a downward-sloping demand curve for a normal good. An inferior good requires careful analysis because the income effect works against the substitution effect.

## Demand derivation

By tracing the consumer’s equilibrium as price changes, we can derive a price-quantity relationship. The exact shape depends on preferences, income, prices, and constraints.
