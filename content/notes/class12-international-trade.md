## Why countries trade

Countries trade because resources, technology, skills, climate, and preferences differ. Trade allows people and firms to specialise and exchange products that would be more costly to produce domestically.

## Absolute and comparative advantage

Absolute advantage means producing more with the same resources. Comparative advantage depends on lower opportunity cost. Trade can benefit both sides when each specialises according to comparative advantage, even if one side is more productive in every activity.

## Gains from trade

Trade can expand consumption possibilities, increase variety, encourage competition, and provide access to inputs and technology. The gains are not always distributed equally, so adjustment costs and affected workers should be considered.

## Trade restrictions

A tariff is a tax on imports. A quota limits the quantity imported. Restrictions can protect selected producers but may raise domestic prices, reduce consumer choice, and create efficiency losses. Their effect depends on market conditions and policy design.

## Balance of payments and exchange rates

A country’s international transactions are recorded in the balance of payments. Exchange rates influence export competitiveness, import costs, remittances, and the domestic price of traded goods.
