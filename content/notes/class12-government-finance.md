## Public finance

Government finance examines how the public sector raises revenue, spends funds, borrows, and manages its budget. The central questions are what services should be provided, who should pay, and how the burden should be shared over time.

## Public revenue

Public revenue comes from tax revenue, non-tax revenue, grants, and borrowing. Direct taxes are imposed on income or wealth, while indirect taxes are collected through transactions. A fair tax system considers ability to pay, simplicity, certainty, and administrative cost.

## Public expenditure

Public expenditure may provide public goods, support social protection, build infrastructure, operate public services, or stabilise the economy. Classifying expenditure helps evaluate whether spending supports current consumption, investment, or redistribution.

## Government budget

A budget compares expected revenue with expected expenditure. A surplus occurs when revenue exceeds expenditure, a deficit occurs when expenditure exceeds revenue, and a balanced budget has equal planned amounts. A deficit may be financed by borrowing or other sources, with implications for future budgets.

## Fiscal policy

Expansionary fiscal policy increases public spending or reduces taxes to support demand. Contractionary fiscal policy reduces spending growth or increases taxes to ease demand pressure. The final effect depends on the size of the multiplier, financing, capacity constraints, and the response of households and firms.
