## Overview

In the **short run**, a firm cannot change its fixed capital equipment, factory buildings, or heavy machinery. Production can only be expanded by employing more variable inputs (raw materials, daily labour, electricity). This technological constraint produces distinct geometric shapes for short-run total and average cost curves.

## Part 1: Derivation of Short-Run Total Cost Curves ($TFC, TVC, TC$)

### Q1. Explain the shapes and derivation of $TFC, TVC$, and $TC$ curves with a diagram.
**Answer:**

![Derivation and Shapes of Short-Run Total Cost Curves: TFC, TVC, and TC](/images/uploads/short-run-total-cost-curves-tfc-tvc-tc.svg)

![class12 cost short run curves diagram 2](/flowcharts/class-12/class12-cost-short-run-curves-diagram-2.svg)

#### Detailed Explanation:
1. **Total Fixed Cost ($TFC$) Curve:**
   * Starts at a positive point on the vertical cost axis (e.g., Rs. 60) and runs as a **horizontal straight line parallel to the horizontal output axis**.
   * It indicates that whether output is 0, 10, or 100 units, fixed overheads remain unchanged.
2. **Total Variable Cost ($TVC$) Curve:**
   * Starts at the **origin $(0, 0)$** because when output is zero, variable cost is zero.
   * It has an **inverted S-shape**:
     * *Initially (at low output):* $TVC$ increases at a **decreasing rate** (concave downward) because of increasing returns to the variable factor.
     * *Later (at high output):* $TVC$ increases at an **increasing rate** (convex upward) because of diminishing returns to the variable factor.
3. **Total Cost ($TC$) Curve:**
   * Starts from the **positive intercept $(0, TFC)$ on the vertical axis**, not the origin, because $TC = TFC$ when $Q = 0$.
   * It is derived by vertically adding $TFC$ to $TVC$ at every output level.
   * Because $TFC$ is constant, the $TC$ curve is **strictly parallel to the $TVC$ curve**, and the vertical distance between $TC$ and $TVC$ is always constant and equal to $TFC$.

## Part 2: Derivation of Per-Unit Cost Curves ($AFC, AVC, AC, MC$)

### Q2. How are $AFC, AVC, AC$, and $MC$ curves derived geometrically?
**Answer:**

![class12 cost short run curves diagram 3](/flowcharts/class-12/class12-cost-short-run-curves-diagram-3.svg)

#### 1. Derivation of Average Fixed Cost ($AFC$) Curve:
* $AFC = \frac{TFC}{Q}$.
* As $Q$ increases, $AFC$ falls continuously.
* Geometrically, the area under the $AFC$ curve at any point is $AFC \times Q = TFC = \text{Constant}$.
* Therefore, the $AFC$ curve takes the shape of a **Rectangular Hyperbola** that slopes downward from left to right, coming close to both axes without ever touching them.

#### 2. Derivation of Average Variable Cost ($AVC$) Curve:
* $AVC = \frac{TVC}{Q}$.
* Geometrically, $AVC$ at any output point on the $TVC$ curve is equal to the **slope of a straight ray drawn from the origin $(0,0)$ to that point on the $TVC$ curve**.
* As output increases, the slope of the ray initially decreases, reaches a minimum, and then increases, giving the $AVC$ curve its **U-shape**.

#### 3. Derivation of Average Total Cost ($AC$) Curve:
* $AC = \frac{TC}{Q} = AFC + AVC$.
* Geometrically, $AC$ at any point is the **slope of a straight ray drawn from the origin $(0,0)$ to that point on the $TC$ curve**, or the vertical summation of the $AFC$ and $AVC$ curves.
* It is **U-shaped**, reaching its lowest point to the right of the lowest point of $AVC$.

#### 4. Derivation of Marginal Cost ($MC$) Curve:
* $MC = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q} = \frac{\Delta TVC}{\Delta Q}$.
* Geometrically, $MC$ at any output is equal to the **slope of the tangent line drawn to the $TC$ or $TVC$ curve** at that output.
* Because $TC$ and $TVC$ are parallel, their tangents have identical slopes at any output level.
* As output increases, the tangent slope initially flattens (falling $MC$), reaches a point of inflection (minimum $MC$), and then gets progressively steeper (rising $MC$), creating the **U-shaped $MC$ curve**.

## Part 3: Summary Table of Short-Run Cost Curves

---

### Q3. Complete Summary of Short-Run Cost Curves

| Cost Curve | Starting Point | Geometric Shape | Economic Cause of Shape |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Fixed Cost ($TFC$)** | $(0, \text{Positive Intercept})$ | Horizontal Straight Line | Fixed factors do not change with output in the short run. |
| **Total Variable Cost ($TVC$)** | Origin $(0, 0)$ | Inverted S-Shape | Law of Variable Proportions (Increasing then Diminishing Returns). |
| **Total Cost ($TC$)** | $(0, TFC)$ on Y-axis | Inverted S-Shape (Parallel to $TVC$) | Vertical sum of $TFC$ and $TVC$; constant vertical gap $= TFC$. |
| **Average Fixed Cost ($AFC$)** | High on Y-axis | Rectangular Hyperbola | Constant $TFC$ spread over expanding output ($AFC \times Q = \text{const}$). |
| **Average Variable Cost ($AVC$)** | Starts at $P_1$ | U-Shaped | Law of Variable Proportions (Initially falls, reaches min, then rises). |
| **Average Total Cost ($AC$)** | Starts above $AVC$ | U-Shaped (Above $AVC$) | Combined effect of falling $AFC$ and U-shaped $AVC$ ($AC = AFC + AVC$). |
| **Marginal Cost ($MC$)** | Starts with $AVC$ | Sharp U-Shaped | Changes in variable factor productivity; cuts $AVC$ & $AC$ at their minimums. |

## Quick Revision Check

1. **Why does the $TC$ curve not start from the origin?**  
   *Because at zero output, fixed costs still exist ($TC = TFC > 0$).*
2. **What is the mathematical reason why the $TC$ and $TVC$ curves are parallel?**  
   *Because the vertical difference between them is Total Fixed Cost ($TC - TVC = TFC$), which is constant at all output levels.*
3. **What is the geometric representation of $MC$ on a $TC$ graph?**  
   *The slope of the tangent line to the $TC$ (or $TVC$) curve at that output level.*
4. **Why does the minimum point of $AC$ occur at a higher output than the minimum point of $AVC$?**  
   *Because falling $AFC$ continues to drag $AC$ downward even after $AVC$ has started to rise.*
