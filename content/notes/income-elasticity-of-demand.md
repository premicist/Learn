## 1. What is Income Elasticity of Demand?

**Income Elasticity of Demand ($E_y$)** measures the degree of responsiveness of the quantity demanded of a commodity to a change in **consumer income**, holding all prices constant (*ceteris paribus*).

$$E_y = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Demanded}}{\% \text{ Change in Consumer Income}} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta Y} \times \frac{Y_1}{Q_1}$$

* $Y_1$ = Initial consumer income, $Y_2$ = New consumer income, $\Delta Y = Y_2 - Y_1$
* $Q_1$ = Initial quantity demanded, $Q_2$ = New quantity demanded, $\Delta Q = Q_2 - Q_1$

## 2. Classification of Goods Based on Income Elasticity ($E_y$)

Economists use the sign and magnitude of $E_y$ to classify commodities into distinct categories:

![income elasticity of demand diagram 1](/flowcharts/introduction-to-economics/income-elasticity-of-demand-diagram-1.svg)

### 1. Luxury Goods ($E_y > 1$ — High Positive Elasticity)
* **Behavior:** When consumer income rises by 10%, the demand for luxury goods increases by **more than 10%**.
* **Examples:** Five-star luxury hotel suites, sports cars, overseas vacation tours, and fine jewelry.

### 2. Necessity / Normal Goods ($0 < E_y < 1$ — Low Positive Elasticity)
* **Behavior:** Demand rises as income increases, but at a **slower rate** than the rise in income (aligned with *Engel's Law of Consumption*).
* **Examples:** Staple foods (rice, flour), basic clothing, household utilities, and domestic healthcare.

### 3. Inferior Goods ($E_y < 0$ — Negative Elasticity)
* **Behavior:** When consumer income rises, buyers feel richer and **reduce** their consumption of cheap inferior goods, switching to higher-quality substitutes.
* **Examples:** Coarse cereals (millet/bajra), low-cost secondhand goods, and basic budget shared dormitories.

### 4. Inexpensive Essentials ($E_y = 0$ — Zero Elasticity)
* **Behavior:** Quantity demanded remains completely unchanged regardless of whether consumer income increases or decreases.
* **Examples:** Table salt, matchboxes, and basic postcards.

## 3. Summary Table: Goods by Income Elasticity

| Category of Good | Income Elasticity ($E_y$) | Demand Response to Income Rise ($Y \uparrow$) | Practical Industry Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Luxury Goods** | $E_y > 1$ (High Positive) | Demand increases faster than income ($\% \Delta Q > \% \Delta Y$) | Premium resorts, sports cars, designer fashion |
| **Necessities (Normal)** | $0 < E_y < 1$ (Low Positive) | Demand increases slower than income ($\% \Delta Q < \% \Delta Y$) | Rice, milk, cooking oil, basic utilities |
| **Inexpensive Essentials** | $E_y = 0$ (Zero Elasticity) | Demand remains completely unchanged | Table salt, matchboxes |
| **Inferior Goods** | $E_y < 0$ (Negative Elasticity) | Demand decreases as income rises | Coarse grains, basic budget hostels |

## 4. Managerial Importance of Income Elasticity

Understanding income elasticity is vital for business strategy and forward planning:

1. **Sales Forecasting Across Business Cycles:**  
   During periods of national economic growth (GDP expansion), luxury businesses (resorts, airlines, cars) experience rapid sales growth, while budget/inferior brands may see demand plateau.
2. **Product Portfolio Diversification:**  
   Large hospitality and retail companies maintain diverse brand portfolios spanning luxury tiers (high $E_y$), mid-scale tiers ($E_y \approx 1$), and budget tiers ($E_y < 1$) to stay resilient during recessions.
3. **Location and Marketing Strategy:**  
   High-income elasticity products are positioned in affluent metropolitan areas and luxury retail centers.

## 5. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **What is the value of income elasticity for an inferior good?**  
   *Answer:* Negative ($E_y < 0$).
2. **State Engel's Law regarding income and food expenditure.**  
   *Answer:* As household income increases, the proportion of income spent on food decreases ($0 < E_y < 1$).
3. **If a 5% rise in income causes an 8% increase in demand for flights, what type of good is it?**  
   *Answer:* A luxury good ($E_y = 8\% / 5\% = 1.6 > 1$).

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Define income elasticity of demand and explain how it is used to classify goods.
2. Explain the managerial importance of income elasticity for a hotel business.
