## Meaning and nature of sociology

Sociology is the systematic study of society, social relationships, institutions, and patterns of interaction. It looks beyond individual events to ask how groups, norms, roles, culture, and institutions shape behaviour and opportunities.

Sociological thinking connects personal experiences with wider social conditions. It uses evidence and comparison to examine how social life is organised and how it changes.

## Subject matter and scope

The scope of sociology includes social interaction, groups, communities, institutions, culture, socialisation, inequality, social change, and population. These topics overlap with economics, psychology, anthropology, and political science, but sociology focuses especially on relationships and social patterns.

## Origin and development

Sociology developed as scholars tried to understand major economic, political, and social transformations. Its concepts continue to evolve as societies experience new technologies, institutions, forms of work, and patterns of communication.

## Social interaction

Social interaction is the process through which people respond to one another. Exchange, competition, cooperation, conflict, and coercion are different forms of interaction. A single relationship may include more than one form over time.

## Sociology in business and management

Organisations operate within society. Sociology helps explain workplace culture, customer behaviour, team relationships, leadership, inequality, institutional expectations, and the social effects of business decisions.
