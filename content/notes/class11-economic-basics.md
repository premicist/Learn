## The basic economic problem

Human wants are numerous, but the resources used to satisfy them are limited. **Scarcity** means that available resources are insufficient to satisfy every want at the same time. Because resources are scarce, every person, firm, and government must make choices.

Economics studies how people and institutions use scarce resources among competing uses. A good answer should connect three ideas: **scarcity**, **choice**, and **opportunity cost**.

## Opportunity cost

The opportunity cost of a choice is the value of the next-best alternative that is given up. If a student spends an evening revising economics instead of working a paid shift, the opportunity cost is the value of that shift, assuming it was the next-best alternative.

Opportunity cost is not the list of every alternative. It is the value of the best alternative sacrificed.

## Microeconomics and macroeconomics

**Microeconomics** examines individual consumers, firms, markets, and resource decisions. **Macroeconomics** examines the economy as a whole, including national income, inflation, employment, and economic growth.

The two perspectives are connected. A change in a household’s demand is microeconomic, while a sustained rise in the general price level is macroeconomic.

## Resources and factors of production

The main factors of production are **land**, **labour**, **capital**, and **organisation or entrepreneurship**. Land includes natural resources. Labour is human effort. Capital includes produced means of production such as tools, machines, and buildings. Organisation brings resources together, makes decisions, and accepts business risk.

A useful revision habit is to explain each factor with one local example and one payment received by its owner.
