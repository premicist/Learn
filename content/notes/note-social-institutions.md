## Meaning of social institutions

Social institutions are established patterns of behaviour organised around important needs of society. They provide roles, expectations, relationships, and rules that help people coordinate their lives.

## Family and marriage

Family supports care, early socialisation, identity, and cooperation across generations. Marriage is a socially recognised relationship that may organise partnership, family life, rights, and responsibilities. Their forms and expectations vary across societies and change over time.

## Education

Education transmits knowledge, skills, language, values, and credentials. It can expand opportunity and prepare people for work and citizenship, while unequal access may reproduce differences in social and economic outcomes.

## Religion

Religion can provide shared meaning, moral expectations, identity, community, and support. Its influence on social life varies across groups and historical settings.

## Economic system and politics

Economic institutions organise production, exchange, work, and distribution. Political institutions organise authority, collective decisions, public rules, and the provision of common services. Capitalist and socialist arrangements represent different ways of organising economic life, while real societies can combine different practices.

## Institutions and everyday life

Institutions shape the choices available to people, but they are also changed by social action, technology, demographic shifts, and collective demands. Understanding institutions helps explain workplace expectations, public policy, family life, and community relationships.
