## Bond-price relationship

For a fixed payment, a bond’s market price and its yield move in opposite directions. A higher price implies a lower return for a new buyer, while a lower price implies a higher yield.

## Liquidity preference

When people prefer liquid money balances, the interest rate must compensate them for holding less liquid assets. Expectations about future rates and asset prices affect this choice.

## Liquidity trap

In a liquidity trap, interest rates may be very low and people may be willing to hold additional money rather than buy bonds. Conventional monetary expansion can then have a weaker effect on spending.
