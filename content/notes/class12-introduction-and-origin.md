## Overview

Economics is the study of how individuals, businesses, and societies make decisions when faced with limited resources. This lesson explores the origin of the subject, its historical definitions, and the primary branches of economic analysis.

## Part 1: Origin and Basic Meaning of Economics

### Q1. What is the linguistic origin of the word 'Economics'?
**Answer:**
The word **Economics** comes from the ancient Greek word **'Oikonomia'**, which is formed from two smaller words:
* **'Oikos'** meaning a *house* or *household*.
* **'Nemein'** meaning *to manage* or *to distribute*.

Combined, *Oikonomia* originally meant **"the art of household management"**. Just as a family head must manage a limited monthly budget to fulfill the various needs of family members, an economy must manage its limited resources (land, labour, capital, tools) to meet the needs of all citizens.

---

### Q2. Who is regarded as the 'Father of Economics' and why?
**Answer:**
**Adam Smith** (1723–1790), a Scottish philosopher and economist, is universally recognized as the **Father of Modern Economics**. 

**Reasons:**
1. In **1776**, he published his monumental book entitled *"An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations"* (commonly known as *The Wealth of Nations*).
2. Prior to Adam Smith, economics was studied as a branch of ethics, religion, or political philosophy. Smith established economics as an **independent, systematic academic science**.
3. He introduced foundational economic principles such as the division of labour, the price mechanism, and the concept of the *invisible hand* (how self-interest in a competitive market guides economic resources efficiently).

---

### Q3. Define Economics in simple terms. Why is it classified as a Social Science?
**Answer:**
**Definition:**
> **Economics** is a branch of social science that studies how human beings and societies allocate their **limited resources** to produce, distribute, and consume goods and services to satisfy their **unlimited wants**.

**Why Economics is a Social Science:**
* **Focuses on Human Behaviour:** It investigates the economic actions, choices, and habits of human beings living together in society.
* **Scientific Methodology:** It uses systematic observation, collection of economic data, formulation of theories/hypotheses, and logical deduction to discover general economic laws (such as the Law of Demand or Law of Diminishing Returns).
* **Social Context:** Unlike pure natural sciences (like physics or chemistry) whose experiments occur in controlled laboratories, economic laws operate in a dynamic society where human preferences, tastes, and institutions constantly change.

### Q4. Explain Adam Smith's Wealth Definition of Economics along with its main features and criticisms.
**Answer:**
**Definition (Classical View - 1776):**
In his book *The Wealth of Nations* (1776), Adam Smith defined economics as:
> *"An inquiry into the nature and causes of the wealth of nations."* In simple words, economics is the **science of wealth**.

#### Main Features:
1. **Study of Wealth Only:** Wealth (physical goods that possess market exchange value) is placed at the very centre of economic study.
2. **Concept of 'Economic Man':** Assumes that humans are rational economic actors guided solely by self-interest and the desire to maximize personal wealth.
3. **Primary Focus on Production & Accumulation:** Examines how a country can increase its national wealth through trade, capital accumulation, and division of labour.
4. **Secondary Place to Human Beings:** Wealth is considered the end goal of all human activities.

#### Major Criticisms:
* **Over-Materialistic ('Dismal Science'):** Social critics like John Ruskin and Thomas Carlyle labelled it a *"dismal science"* and *"pig philosophy"* because it seemed to preach selfishness and wealth worship while ignoring human values, love, and moral duty.
* **Neglect of Immaterial Services:** Excluded essential services provided by teachers, doctors, lawyers, and artists because they do not produce physical, tangible goods.
* **Ignored the Problem of Scarcity:** It did not explain how scarcity forces people to make choices.

---

### Q5. Explain Alfred Marshall's Welfare Definition of Economics along with its main features and criticisms.
**Answer:**
**Definition (Neoclassical View - 1890):**
In his book *Principles of Economics* (1890), Alfred Marshall shifted the focus of economics from wealth to **human welfare**:
> *"Economics is a study of mankind in the ordinary business of life; it examines that part of individual and social action which is most closely connected with the attainment and with the use of the material requisites of well-being."*

#### Main Features:
1. **Primary Importance to Man:** Man is placed at the centre of study; wealth is merely a means to achieve human welfare, not an end in itself.
2. **Study of the Ordinary Person:** Studies ordinary individuals engaged in everyday economic activities (earning an income and spending it), excluding isolated ascetics or hermits.
3. **Emphasis on Material Welfare:** Focuses on goods that promote physical well-being (food, clothing, shelter, medicine).
4. **Normative & Social Science:** Examines what promotes human happiness and living standards.

#### Major Criticisms (by Lionel Robbins):
* **Narrow Distinction (Material vs. Immaterial):** Marshall included tangible goods (e.g., bread, clothes) but excluded intangible services (e.g., teaching, medical care) which equally contribute to human well-being.
* **Welfare is Subjective and Immeasurable:** Welfare is a state of mind that varies from person to person, place to place, and time to time. It cannot be counted or measured objectively in numbers.
* **Excludes Non-Welfare Economic Activities:** Economic activities that produce harmful goods (e.g., alcohol, tobacco) involve economic transactions and pricing, but Marshall's definition fails to explain them because they do not promote "welfare".

---

### Q6. Explain Lionel Robbins' Scarcity and Choice Definition of Economics. Why is it considered superior?
**Answer:**
**Definition (Modern Analytical View - 1932):**
In his book *An Essay on the Nature and Significance of Economic Science* (1932), Lionel Robbins provided a scientific and universal definition:
> *"Economics is the science which studies human behaviour as a relationship between ends and scarce means which have alternative uses."*

#### Main Pillars of Robbins' Definition:
1. **Unlimited Ends (Wants):** Human wants are endless. As soon as one desire is satisfied, another arises.
2. **Scarce Means (Resources):** Productive resources (time, money, land, labour, raw materials) are limited in supply relative to demand.
3. **Alternative Uses of Resources:** Scarce resources can be put to multiple different uses (for example, electricity can be used to run factories, light homes, or power vehicles; a plot of land can grow crops or support a factory).
4. **Urgency of Wants & Choice:** Since wants differ in urgency and resources are scarce, people must make **choices** on how to allocate their means.

![class12 introduction and origin diagram 3](/flowcharts/class-12/class12-introduction-and-origin-diagram-3.svg)

#### Why Robbins' Definition is Superior:
* **Universal Applicability:** Applies to all societies—capitalist, socialist, mixed, rich, or poor—because scarcity and choice exist everywhere.
* **Purely Scientific (Positive Approach):** It remains neutral between ends; it analyzes *what is* without passing value judgments on whether a want is good or bad.
* **Includes Both Goods and Services:** It applies equally to physical commodities and intangible services without artificial distinctions.

### Q7. Summary Comparison of the Major Definitions of Economics

| Comparison Criteria | Adam Smith (Wealth View - 1776) | Alfred Marshall (Welfare View - 1890) | Lionel Robbins (Scarcity View - 1932) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Central Focus** | Wealth creation and accumulation | Material human welfare | Scarcity of resources and problem of choice |
| **Position of Human** | Secondary to wealth | Primary; wealth is a means to welfare | Central; studies human decision-making behaviour |
| **Scope of Goods** | Tangible material goods only | Tangible material requisites only | All scarce goods and services with alternative uses |
| **Nature of Science** | Positive / Practical science | Normative science (focuses on well-being) | Pure positive science (neutral between ends) |
| **Applicability** | Limited to commercial wealth | Limited to welfare-promoting material goods | Universal to all economic systems and individuals |

### Q8. Distinguish between Microeconomics and Macroeconomics.

![class12 introduction and origin diagram 4](/flowcharts/class-12/class12-introduction-and-origin-diagram-4.svg)

**Answer:**
The terms *Microeconomics* and *Macroeconomics* were coined by the Norwegian economist **Ragnar Frisch** in 1933.

#### Detailed Comparison:

| Feature | Microeconomics | Macroeconomics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin** | Derived from Greek word *'Mikros'* (meaning small). | Derived from Greek word *'Makros'* (meaning large). |
| **Subject Matter** | Studies the economic behaviour of individual decision-making units (a single consumer, firm, household, or industry). | Studies the economy as a whole and its large-scale aggregates. |
| **Core Objective** | To determine the price of a commodity or factor of production and achieve optimal allocation of resources. | To determine national income, general price level, total output, and overall employment rate. |
| **Central Mechanism / Tool** | **Price Mechanism** (Supply and Demand of individual markets). Often called *Price Theory*. | **Income & Employment Theory** (Aggregate Demand and Aggregate Supply). |
| **Key Variables** | Individual price, individual demand, output of a single firm, wage rate in a specific factory. | Gross Domestic Product (GDP), National Income, Inflation rate, Unemployment rate, Fiscal deficit. |
| **Assumptions** | Assumes macro variables remain constant (e.g., constant national income when studying price of rice). | Assumes micro variables are aggregated (e.g., distribution of individual output is taken as given). |

### Q9. Distinguish between Positive Economics and Normative Economics.

**Answer:**

![class12 introduction and origin diagram 5](/flowcharts/class-12/class12-introduction-and-origin-diagram-5.svg)

| Basis | Positive Economics | Normative Economics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Meaning** | Deals with economic phenomena as they actually are (*"What is"*, *"What was"*, *"What will be"*). | Deals with opinions, ethics, and economic desirability (*"What ought to be"*, *"What should be"*). |
| **Nature** | Purely factual, descriptive, and objective. | Prescriptive, value-based, and subjective. |
| **Verification** | Can be tested, proved, or disproved using empirical data and historical facts. | Cannot be proved or disproved with data because it reflects personal beliefs or policy preferences. |
| **Purpose** | To explain real-world economic causes and effects scientifically. | To formulate economic goals, ideals, and policy prescriptions for social welfare. |
| **Example** | *"A 10% increase in fuel price leads to a 4% decrease in quantity demanded."* | *"The government ought to provide free fuel subsidies to low-income families."* |
