## Ordinal preferences

An ordinal approach ranks bundles from more preferred to less preferred without assigning numerical units of satisfaction. It focuses on the consumer’s ordering of alternatives.

## Indifference curves

An indifference curve joins combinations of two goods that provide the same level of satisfaction. Curves farther from the origin represent higher satisfaction when preferences are well behaved.

## Main properties

Indifference curves are usually downward sloping, do not intersect, and are convex to the origin. Their shape reflects how willing a consumer is to substitute one good for another.

## Marginal rate of substitution

The marginal rate of substitution is the amount of one good a consumer is willing to give up for an additional unit of another while remaining equally satisfied. It is related to the slope of the curve.
