## 1. What is Cross Elasticity of Demand?

**Cross Elasticity of Demand ($E_{xy}$)** measures the percentage change in the quantity demanded of **Good $X$** resulting from a percentage change in the price of a **related Good $Y$**, holding all other factors constant (*ceteris paribus*).

$$E_{xy} = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Demanded of Good X}}{\% \text{ Change in Price of Good Y}} = \frac{\Delta Q_x}{\Delta P_y} \times \frac{P_{y1}}{Q_{x1}}$$

* $P_{y1}$ = Initial price of Good $Y$, $\Delta P_y$ = Change in price of Good $Y$
* $Q_{x1}$ = Initial quantity demanded of Good $X$, $\Delta Q_x$ = Change in quantity demanded of Good $X$

---

## 2. The Three Types of Cross Elasticity

Depending on the economic relationship between the two goods, cross elasticity can be positive, negative, or zero:

![Types of Cross Elasticity of Demand](/images/uploads/cross-elasticity-types.svg)

### 1. Positive Cross Elasticity ($E_{xy} > 0$) — Substitute Goods
* **Mechanism:** When the price of Good $Y$ (Coffee) rises, consumers switch away from Coffee and buy more Good $X$ (Tea).
* **Curve Direction:** Slopes **upward from left to right** (positive slope).
* **Examples:** Tea vs. Coffee; Coca-Cola vs. Pepsi; Resort Hotel A vs. Resort Hotel B.

### 2. Negative Cross Elasticity ($E_{xy} < 0$) — Complementary Goods
* **Mechanism:** When the price of Good $Y$ (Automobiles) rises, fewer cars are purchased, which automatically reduces the demand for Good $X$ (Petrol).
* **Curve Direction:** Slopes **downward from left to right** (negative slope).
* **Examples:** Petrol and Cars; Hotel Room Stays and Restaurant Dining; Printers and Ink Cartridges.

### 3. Zero Cross Elasticity ($E_{xy} = 0$) — Unrelated Goods
* **Mechanism:** A change in the price of Good $Y$ has no effect on the demand for Good $X$.
* **Curve Direction:** A **vertical straight line** parallel to the $Y$-axis.
* **Examples:** Price of automobiles and demand for table salt; price of shoes and demand for milk.

## 3. Summary Table: Cross Elasticity Types

| Type of Related Good | Value of Cross Elasticity | Slope of Cross-Demand Curve | Demand Effect | Real-World Business Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Substitute Goods** | **Positive ($E_{xy} > 0$)** | Upward Sloping | Price of $Y \uparrow \implies$ Demand for $X \uparrow$ | Tea and Coffee; Coke and Pepsi |
| **Complementary Goods** | **Negative ($E_{xy} < 0$)** | Downward Sloping | Price of $Y \uparrow \implies$ Demand for $X \downarrow$ | Hotel Rooms and Dining; Cars and Petrol |
| **Unrelated Goods** | **Zero ($E_{xy} = 0$)** | Vertical Line | Price of $Y$ has zero impact on $X$ | Butter and Shoes; Salt and Laptops |

## 4. Managerial Importance of Cross Elasticity

1. **Competitor Pricing and Market Boundary:**  
   Helps businesses identify their true competitors. A high positive $E_{xy}$ indicates close substitutes, meaning a price cut by a rival will heavily impact your sales.
2. **Product Bundling and Joint Pricing:**  
   Hotels and service businesses use complementary pricing strategies (e.g., offering discounted room tariffs while earning revenue on food, spa, and banquet services).
3. **Antitrust and Merger Regulation:**  
   Governments use cross elasticity to evaluate whether two merging companies would dominate a single product market.

## 5. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **What is the sign of cross elasticity for substitute goods?**  
   *Answer:* Positive ($E_{xy} > 0$).
2. **Give an example of goods with negative cross elasticity.**  
   *Answer:* Hotel room bookings and hotel restaurant dining (or automobiles and petrol).
3. **What does a cross elasticity of zero ($E_{xy} = 0$) signify?**  
   *Answer:* The two goods are completely unrelated in consumption.

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Explain the concept of cross elasticity of demand and distinguish its three types with diagrams.
2. How do hotel managers utilize cross elasticity to set menu prices and room package bundles?
