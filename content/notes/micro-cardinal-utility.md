## Utility and measurement

Utility means the satisfaction a consumer obtains from consumption. A cardinal approach treats utility as measurable for analytical purposes, even though real satisfaction is difficult to observe directly.

## Total and marginal utility

Total utility is satisfaction from all units consumed. Marginal utility is the additional satisfaction from one more unit. Marginal utility often falls as consumption increases, which is the law of diminishing marginal utility.

## Equi-marginal principle

A consumer allocates a limited budget so that the marginal utility per unit of money is equal across uses, subject to available choices and prices.

## Consumer equilibrium

Equilibrium is reached when the consumer has no beneficial rearrangement of spending under the model’s assumptions. Changes in price, income, tastes, or available goods can alter the result.
