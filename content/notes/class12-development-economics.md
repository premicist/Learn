## Growth and development

Economic growth refers to an increase in real output or real income. Economic development is broader: it considers living standards, capabilities, distribution, employment, health, education, and sustainability. Growth can support development, but it does not guarantee it.

## Poverty

Poverty can be measured through income, consumption, or a wider set of deprivations. A poverty line is useful for comparison, but it does not capture every aspect of insecurity, access, or vulnerability.

## Inequality

Inequality describes how income, wealth, opportunities, or services are distributed. Average income may rise while inequality also rises, so distributional evidence should accompany growth figures.

## Unemployment

Unemployment means that people who are able and willing to work cannot find work. Its forms include frictional, structural, seasonal, and cyclical unemployment. Underemployment and informal work also matter when assessing employment conditions.

## Human resources

Education, health, skills, and experience improve human capabilities and can raise productivity. Investment in human resources can support both individual opportunity and long-run development.

## A balanced development answer

A strong evaluation combines an indicator with its limitation, identifies who benefits, considers employment and human resources, and recognises environmental or institutional constraints. This approach avoids treating one statistic as the whole story.

Explain why a rise in real GDP may occur without an equal improvement in development. Include one point about distribution and one point about human resources.
