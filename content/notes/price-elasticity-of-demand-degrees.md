## 1. What is Price Elasticity of Demand?

**Price Elasticity of Demand ($E_p$)** measures the degree of responsiveness of the quantity demanded of a commodity to a change in its **own price**, holding other factors constant (*ceteris paribus*).

$$E_p = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Demanded}}{\% \text{ Change in Price}} = -\left(\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}\right)$$

* If a small price change leads to a large change in quantity demanded, demand is **elastic**.
* If a large price change leads to only a small change in quantity demanded, demand is **inelastic**.

## 2. The Five Degrees of Price Elasticity of Demand

Economists classify price elasticity into **five distinct degrees**:

![Five Degrees of Price Elasticity of Demand](/images/uploads/five-degrees-price-elasticity.svg)

### 1. Perfectly Inelastic Demand ($E_p = 0$)
* **Concept:** Quantity demanded does not change at all, regardless of how much the price rises or falls ($\% \Delta Q = 0$).
* **Shape of Curve:** A **vertical straight line** parallel to the $Y$-axis.
* **Real-World Examples:** Life-saving prescription medicines (insulin for diabetes), emergency medical procedures, and salt.

### 2. Relatively Inelastic Demand ($E_p < 1$)
* **Concept:** The percentage change in quantity demanded is **less than** the percentage change in price ($\% \Delta Q < \% \Delta P$).
* **Shape of Curve:** A **steep downward-sloping** curve.
* **Real-World Examples:** Basic food staples (rice, wheat), electricity, cooking gas, and daily commuting public transit.

### 3. Unitary Elastic Demand ($E_p = 1$)
* **Concept:** The percentage change in quantity demanded is **exactly equal** to the percentage change in price ($\% \Delta Q = \% \Delta P$).
* **Shape of Curve:** A **Rectangular Hyperbola** curve, where Total Revenue ($TR = P \times Q$) remains constant at all price levels.
* **Real-World Examples:** Mid-tier branded clothing, standard electronics, and packaged consumer goods.

### 4. Relatively Elastic Demand ($E_p > 1$)
* **Concept:** The percentage change in quantity demanded is **greater than** the percentage change in price ($\% \Delta Q > \% \Delta P$).
* **Shape of Curve:** A **flatter downward-sloping** curve.
* **Real-World Examples:** Consumer durables (LED televisions, motorbikes, smartphones) and luxury hotel stays where substitutes are easily available.

### 5. Perfectly Elastic Demand ($E_p = \infty$)
* **Concept:** Buyers are willing to purchase an infinite quantity at a specific price, but if the price rises even slightly, quantity demanded drops to zero.
* **Shape of Curve:** A **horizontal straight line** parallel to the $X$-axis.
* **Real-World Examples:** The demand curve facing an individual agricultural farmer or firm operating under **Perfect Competition**.

## 3. Comprehensive Summary Table: The Five Degrees

| Degree of Elasticity | Coefficient Value | Shape of Demand Curve | Percentage Relationship | Real-World & Business Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Perfectly Inelastic** | $E_p = 0$ | Vertical straight line | $\% \Delta Q = 0$ despite large $\% \Delta P$ | Life-saving insulin, emergency healthcare |
| **2. Relatively Inelastic** | $E_p < 1$ | Steep downward slope | $\% \Delta Q < \% \Delta P$ | Rice, wheat, electricity, water utility |
| **3. Unitary Elastic** | $E_p = 1$ | Rectangular Hyperbola | $\% \Delta Q = \% \Delta P$ (TR is constant) | General consumer goods, footwear |
| **4. Relatively Elastic** | $E_p > 1$ | Flat downward slope | $\% \Delta Q > \% \Delta P$ | LED TVs, motorbikes, resort hotels |
| **5. Perfectly Elastic** | $E_p = \infty$ | Horizontal straight line | Infinite quantity at fixed market price | Standardized wheat in perfect competition |

## 4. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **What is the shape of a perfectly inelastic demand curve?**  
   *Answer:* A vertical straight line parallel to the price ($Y$) axis.
2. **If a 4% decrease in price causes an 8% increase in quantity demanded, what is the elasticity?**  
   *Answer:* $E_p = 8\% / 4\% = 2$ (Relatively Elastic, $E_p > 1$).
3. **What happens to Total Revenue when demand is Unitary Elastic ($E_p = 1$)?**  
   *Answer:* Total Revenue remains constant when price changes.

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Explain the five degrees of price elasticity of demand with schedules and diagrams.
2. Why do luxury goods typically have elastic demand while necessities have inelastic demand?
