## 1. What is a Pure Monopoly?

A **Pure Monopoly** is a market structure in which a **single seller or producing firm** supplies the entire market with a product that has **no close substitutes**, protected by **absolute barriers to entry**.

In a monopoly, the firm is the entire industry ($\text{Firm} = \text{Industry}$).

![monopoly and price discrimination diagram 1](/flowcharts/introduction-to-economics/monopoly-and-price-discrimination-diagram-1.svg)

### Sources of Entry Barriers:
1. **Natural Monopolies:** Enormous economies of scale make a single large network utility (e.g., city water pipelines, national electricity grid) far cheaper per unit than multiple competing firms.
2. **Legal and Intellectual Barriers:** Government patents, exclusive copyrights, and operating franchises.
3. **Control over Essential Raw Materials:** Exclusive ownership of vital physical resources (e.g., De Beers diamond mines).

## 2. Price and Output Determination in Monopoly

Because the monopolist faces the entire downward-sloping market demand curve ($AR$), Marginal Revenue lies strictly below Average Revenue ($MR < AR$):

![Monopoly Price & Output Determination](/images/uploads/monopoly-equilibrium-profit.svg)

### Profit-Maximizing Steps:
1. **First-Order Condition:** The firm identifies the output level $Q^*$ where **Marginal Revenue equals Marginal Cost ($MR = MC$)**.
2. **Setting Market Price ($P^*$):** The monopolist projects upward from $Q^*$ to the **Demand Curve ($AR$)** to determine the maximum price consumers are willing to pay ($P^* > MC$).
3. **Calculating Supernormal Profit:**
   $$\text{Profit } (\pi) = (P^* - AC) \times Q^*$$
   *(Graphically represented by the shaded rectangle $P^* A B C$ in the diagram).*

> **Persistent Long-Run Profits:** Unlike perfect competition where new firms enter and eliminate profits, high entry barriers allow a monopolist to sustain **supernormal profits even in the long run**.

## 3. Economic Inefficiencies and Deadweight Loss

Compared to perfect competition, a monopoly causes economic losses to society:
1. **Allocative Inefficiency ($P > MC$):** Price exceeds marginal cost, meaning society places higher value on extra output than it costs to produce.
2. **Deadweight Loss ($DWL$):** Under-production reduces total consumer and producer surplus.
3. **Productive Inefficiency:** Monopolists often operate with excess capacity and do not produce at the minimum point of the $LAC$ curve.

## 4. Theory of Price Discrimination (A.C. Pigou)

**Price Discrimination** occurs when a seller charges different prices to different buyers for identical goods or services for reasons not associated with cost differences.

### Necessary Conditions for Price Discrimination:
* The firm must have **market power (Price Maker)**.
* The firm must be able to **segment markets** with differing price elasticities of demand.
* The firm must be able to **prevent resale** between market segments.

---

## 5. The Three Degrees of Price Discrimination

| Degree of Discrimination | Pricing Mechanism | Consumer Surplus Status | Real-World & Business Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First-Degree (Perfect)** | Monopolist charges each buyer their exact **maximum willingness to pay**. | **Zero ($CS = 0$)**; entire consumer surplus captured as profit. | Private auctions, professional consulting fees, personalized medical fees. |
| **Second-Degree (Block Pricing)** | Charges different prices for **different blocks or quantities** consumed. | Partial consumer surplus captured. | Electricity and water utility slab rates, bulk wholesale discounts. |
| **Third-Degree (Market Segmentation)** | Divides market into sub-markets based on **differing price elasticities ($E_d$)**. | Consumer surplus split across groups. | Student/senior citizen bus discounts, peak vs. off-peak hotel room rates. |

### Pricing Rule for Third-Degree Discrimination:
$$\dfrac{P_A}{P_B} = \dfrac{1 - \dfrac{1}{|E_B|}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{|E_A|}}$$

* Charge a **higher price ($P_A$)** in the market with **inelastic demand** ($|E_A| < 1$).
* Charge a **lower price ($P_B$)** in the market with **elastic demand** ($|E_B| > 1$).

## 6. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **What is the primary defining feature of a monopoly?**  
   *Answer:* A single seller of a product with no close substitutes, protected by high barriers to entry.
2. **Why is Marginal Revenue below Average Revenue in a monopoly?**  
   *Answer:* Because the demand curve slopes downward; the monopolist must lower the price on all units to sell more.
3. **What is Third-Degree Price Discrimination?**  
   *Answer:* Charging different prices in different market segments based on differences in price elasticity of demand.

### Long Answer Questions (8-10 Marks):
1. Explain price and output determination under monopoly with a diagram. Why can a monopoly earn supernormal profits in the long run?
2. Discuss A.C. Pigou's three degrees of price discrimination with practical industry examples.
