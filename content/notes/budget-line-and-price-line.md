## 1. What is a Budget Line?

While an indifference curve represents what a consumer *wishes* to consume, the **Budget Line (or Price Line)** represents what the consumer **can afford to purchase** given their limited money income and prevailing market prices.

> **Definition:** The **Budget Line** is the geometric locus of all possible combinations of two commodities (Good X and Good Y) that a consumer can purchase by spending their entire money income ($M$) at given market prices ($P_x, P_y$).

---

## 2. Algebraic Equation of the Budget Line

The total consumer budget constraint is expressed as:

$$M = P_x \cdot X + P_y \cdot Y$$

* $M$ = Total nominal money income of the consumer
* $P_x$ = Market price per unit of Good X
* $P_y$ = Market price per unit of Good Y
* $X, Y$ = Quantities of Good X and Good Y purchased

Rearranging into the standard linear slope-intercept form ($Y = mx + c$):

$$P_y \cdot Y = M - P_x \cdot X$$

$$Y = \dfrac{M}{P_y} - \left(\dfrac{P_x}{P_y}\right) X$$

---

## 3. Intercepts and Slope of the Budget Line

```
Good Y (Units)
  ^
M/Py | A (Vertical Intercept: All Income Spent on Y)
     |  \
     |   \  Budget Line AB
     |    \  Slope = - Px / Py (Price Ratio)
     |     \
   0 +------\------------------> Good X (Units)
             B (M/Px: Horizontal Intercept: All Income Spent on X)
```

1. **Vertical Intercept ($Y$-intercept at Point A):**  
   If the consumer spends 100% of their income on Good Y ($X = 0$):
   $$Y_{\text{max}} = \dfrac{M}{P_y}$$
2. **Horizontal Intercept ($X$-intercept at Point B):**  
   If the consumer spends 100% of their income on Good X ($Y = 0$):
   $$X_{\text{max}} = \dfrac{M}{P_x}$$
3. **Slope of the Budget Line:**  
   The slope equals the vertical drop divided by the horizontal run:
   $$\text{Slope} = -\dfrac{\text{Vertical Intercept}}{\text{Horizontal Intercept}} = -\dfrac{M/P_y}{M/P_x} = -\dfrac{P_x}{P_y}$$
   > **Key Principle:** The slope of the budget line is strictly equal to the **negative price ratio** of the two goods ($-\frac{P_x}{P_y}$), representing the market rate of exchange and the relative opportunity cost of Good X in terms of Good Y.

---

## 4. Shifts and Rotations of the Budget Line

The position and slope of the budget line change whenever consumer income or market prices change:

![Budget Line Shifts and Rotations](/images/uploads/budget-line-shifts-rotations.svg)

### A. Parallel Shifts (Due to Income Changes)
* When **money income rises ($M \uparrow$)** with prices constant, the budget line shifts **parallel outward to the right** ($AB \to A_1B_1$). The consumer can now afford more of both goods.
* When **money income falls ($M \downarrow$)**, the budget line shifts **parallel inward to the left** ($AB \to A_2B_2$).
* *Slope Status:* The slope ($-\frac{P_x}{P_y}$) remains completely unchanged because prices have not changed.

### B. Rotations / Pivots (Due to Price Changes)
* When the **price of Good X falls ($P_x \downarrow$)**, the $X$-intercept moves outward to the right while the $Y$-intercept stays fixed at $A$. The budget line **rotates outward flatter** ($AB \to AB_1$).
* When the **price of Good X rises ($P_x \uparrow$)**, the budget line **rotates inward steeper** ($AB \to AB_2$).
* When the **price of Good Y changes ($P_y$)**, the budget line pivots on the fixed $X$-intercept ($B$).

---

## 5. Summary Table: Budget Line Adjustments

| Market Factor Change | Nature of Movement | Graphical Outcome | Slope Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Income Increases ($M \uparrow$)** | Parallel Shift Outward | Shifts right ($AB \to A_1B_1$) | Slope remains unchanged ($-\frac{P_x}{P_y}$) |
| **Income Decreases ($M \downarrow$)** | Parallel Shift Inward | Shifts left ($AB \to A_2B_2$) | Slope remains unchanged ($-\frac{P_x}{P_y}$) |
| **Price of X Falls ($P_x \downarrow$)** | Outward Pivot on X-axis | X-intercept moves right ($M/P_x \uparrow$) | Flatter slope (slope decreases in absolute value) |
| **Price of X Rises ($P_x \uparrow$)** | Inward Pivot on X-axis | X-intercept moves left ($M/P_x \downarrow$) | Steeper slope (slope increases in absolute value) |
| **Price of Y Falls ($P_y \downarrow$)** | Outward Pivot on Y-axis | Y-intercept moves up ($M/P_y \uparrow$) | Steeper slope |
| **Equal % Rise in Px and Py** | Parallel Shift Inward | Identical to a direct cut in income | Slope remains unchanged |

## 6. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **What is the algebraic equation of a budget line?**  
   *Answer:* $M = P_x \cdot X + P_y \cdot Y$.
2. **What does the slope of the budget line represent?**  
   *Answer:* The negative ratio of market prices ($-\frac{P_x}{P_y}$), representing the relative opportunity cost of Good X.
3. **What happens to the budget line if consumer income doubles while prices stay constant?**  
   *Answer:* The budget line shifts parallel outward to the right.

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Define the budget line and derive its slope algebraically.
2. Distinguish between a parallel shift and a rotation of the budget line with diagrams.
