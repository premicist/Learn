## 1. The 3Cs Pricing Compass & Manager's Decision Framework

Every commercial pricing decision sits at the intersection of three fundamental market forces known as the **3Cs of Pricing**:

| Pillar of 3Cs | Economic Meaning | Managerial Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Cost (Price Floor)** | Average Variable Cost ($AVC$) | Sets the absolute minimum price below which the firm incurs operational cash losses. |
| **2. Customer (Price Ceiling)** | Maximum Willingness to Pay ($WTP$) | Sets the maximum price ceiling that consumers and market demand will tolerate. |
| **3. Competition (Price Range)** | Competitor Prices &amp; Market Structure | Determines the feasible pricing range and tactical positioning against rival firms. |

---

### Manager's Pricing Strategy Decision Guide:

| Business Situation &amp; Operational Challenge | Recommended Pricing Strategy | Core Principle &amp; Formula |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Standard Daily Production** (Retail, food, manufacturing) | **Cost-Plus / Mark-Up Pricing** | Add profit mark-up to unit cost: $P = AVC(1 + m)$ |
| **2. Buyers Have Differing Price Elasticities** | **3rd-Degree Price Discrimination** | Inelastic demand pays high price; Elastic demand pays low price |
| **3. Non-Storable Fixed Capacity Services** (Hotels, power) | **Peak-Load Pricing** | High price during peak congestion; Discount price off-peak |
| **4. Launching an Innovative / High-Tech Product** | **Price Skimming Strategy** | High initial price to recoup R&amp;D $\rightarrow$ gradual price cuts |
| **5. Entering a Competitive Mass Market** | **Penetration Pricing Strategy** | Ultra-low initial price to build volume and scale economies |
| **6. Transfers Between Internal Corporate Divisions** | **Transfer Pricing ($P_t = MC_u$)** | Set internal price at upstream marginal cost to prevent double markup |

## 2. Complete Pricing Formula Cheat-Sheet

| Formula Name | Algebraic Equation | Detailed Explanation of Variables |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1. Cost-Plus Selling Price** | $P = \text{AVC}(1 + m)$ | <ul><li>$P$ = Selling Price per unit</li><li>$\text{AVC}$ = Average Variable Cost per unit</li><li>$m$ = Percentage mark-up on cost (in decimal)</li></ul> |
| **2. Mark-Up on Cost** | $m = \dfrac{P - \text{AVC}}{\text{AVC}} \times 100\%$ | <ul><li>$m$ = Mark-up percentage on cost</li><li>$P$ = Selling Price, $\text{AVC}$ = Unit variable cost</li></ul> |
| **3. Profit Margin on Price** | $\text{Margin} = \dfrac{P - \text{AVC}}{P} \times 100\%$ | <ul><li>$\text{Margin}$ = Percentage of selling price that represents profit</li><li>$P$ = Selling Price, $\text{AVC}$ = Unit variable cost</li></ul> |
| **4. Optimal Monopoly Price** | $P = \dfrac{\text{MC}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{\lvert E_d \rvert}}$ | <ul><li>$P$ = Profit-maximizing price</li><li>$\text{MC}$ = Marginal Cost of production</li><li>$\lvert E_d \rvert$ = Absolute price elasticity of demand</li></ul> |
| **5. Lerner Index of Market Power** | $\dfrac{P - \text{MC}}{P} = \dfrac{1}{\lvert E_d \rvert}$ | <ul><li>Measures pricing power and degree of monopoly above marginal cost</li><li>$P$ = Selling Price, $\text{MC}$ = Marginal Cost</li><li>$\lvert E_d \rvert$ = Absolute price elasticity of demand</li></ul> |
| **6. 3rd-Degree Price Ratio Rule** | $\dfrac{P_A}{P_B} = \dfrac{1 - \dfrac{1}{\lvert E_B \rvert}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{\lvert E_A \rvert}}$ | <ul><li>$P_A, P_B$ = Prices charged in sub-markets A and B</li><li>$\lvert E_A \rvert, \lvert E_B \rvert$ = Price elasticities of demand in markets A and B</li></ul> |
| **7. Transfer Price (No External Market)** | $P_t = \text{MC}_u$ | <ul><li>$P_t$ = Internal transfer price between divisions</li><li>$\text{MC}_u$ = Upstream division's marginal production cost</li></ul> |
| **8. Transfer Price (Competitive Market)** | $P_t = P_m$ | <ul><li>$P_t$ = Internal transfer price</li><li>$P_m$ = Open competitive external market price</li></ul> |

## 3. Master Taxonomy of Pricing Strategies

| Pricing Method | Primary Driver | Market Scenario | Real-World &amp; Industry Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cost-Plus Pricing** | Unit variable cost + profit margin | Retail, manufacturing, food service | Restaurant menu pricing ($P = AVC \times 1.40$) |
| **1st-Degree Discrimination** | Maximum buyer willingness to pay | Perfect auctions, custom consulting | High-end art auctions, bespoke architectural fees |
| **2nd-Degree Discrimination** | Quantity consumed / Block volume | Utilities, wholesale trade | Bulk discount pricing, electricity slab tariffs |
| **3rd-Degree Discrimination** | Differences in buyer elasticity ($E_d$) | Airlines, cinema, transport | Student/senior discounts, domestic vs. foreign fares |
| **Peak-Load Pricing** | Fixed capacity &amp; time fluctuations | Non-storable utilities, hospitality | High-season hotel room tariffs, evening electricity rates |
| **Price Skimming** | High initial willingness to pay | Innovative products with entry barriers | Apple flagship iPhones, Sony PlayStation launches |
| **Penetration Pricing** | Highly elastic mass market demand | Competitive mass markets | Reliance Jio 4G, low-cost budget airlines (AirAsia) |
| **Transfer Pricing** | Upstream marginal cost ($MC_u$) | Vertically integrated corporations | Automobile engine plant transferring to assembly line |

## 4. Practical Real-World Case Snippets

### 📱 Case 1: Apple Inc. — Dual Strategy (Skimming + Premium Mark-Up)
* **Flagship Launch (Price Skimming):** When Apple launches a new flagship iPhone, it sets a premium price (e.g., \$1,200) to capture consumer surplus from tech enthusiasts and early adopters ($|E_d| < 1$). Over time, it introduces budget models (SE series) and discounts older models to capture mass-market consumers.
* **Accessories (High Mark-Up Pricing):** For phone cases, cables, and adapters where consumers exhibit brand loyalty and low price sensitivity, Apple applies mark-ups exceeding 200–300% over manufacturing costs.

### ✈️ Case 2: Airline Industry — 3rd-Degree Discrimination + Peak-Load Pricing
* **Market Segmentation (3rd-Degree):** Airlines charge business travelers high fares for last-minute weekday flights (inelastic demand, employer pays) and offer discounted non-refundable fares to leisure vacationers booking 2 months in advance (elastic demand).
* **Peak-Load Management:** Friday evening and Sunday holiday flights carry premium tariffs because flight seats cannot be stored and capacity is fixed.

### 📶 Case 3: Reliance Jio — Aggressive Market Penetration
* **Launch Phase:** Jio entered the Indian telecom market offering free high-speed 4G data and voice calls for months.
* **Scale Economies:** It attracted over 100 million subscribers rapidly, building massive network scale and forcing rivals to consolidate. Once market dominance was secured, it gradually adjusted tariffs upward.

## 5. Five Solved Model Exam Questions

### 📌 Question 1: Mark-Up and Profit Margin Calculation
**Problem:** A hotel banquet hall prepares catering packages at an average variable cost ($\text{AVC}$) of **Rs. 800 per plate**. Management applies a **25% mark-up on cost**.
1. Calculate the selling price per plate.
2. Calculate the profit margin percentage on the selling price.

#### 💡 Solution:
1. **Selling Price ($P$):**
   $$P = \text{AVC}(1 + m) = 800 \times (1 + 0.25) = 800 \times 1.25 = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 1,000}$$
2. **Profit Margin on Price:**
   $$\text{Margin} = \left(\dfrac{P - \text{AVC}}{P}\right) \times 100\% = \left(\dfrac{1000 - 800}{1000}\right) \times 100\% = \mathbf{20\%}$$

---

### 📌 Question 2: Optimal Monopoly Price from Demand Elasticity
**Problem:** A pharmaceutical manufacturer produces a patented medicine with a constant marginal cost of **Rs. 60 per strip**. The price elasticity of demand is estimated at **$\lvert E_d \rvert = 1.5$**. Calculate the profit-maximizing selling price.

#### 💡 Solution:
$$P = \dfrac{\text{MC}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{\lvert E_d \rvert}} = \dfrac{60}{1 - \dfrac{1}{1.5}} = \dfrac{60}{1 - 0.667} = \dfrac{60}{0.333} = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 180\text{ per strip}}$$

---

### 📌 Question 3: Third-Degree Price Discrimination Ratio
**Problem:** An international airline operates between two market segments:
* **Market A (Business Travelers):** Price elasticity of demand $\lvert E_A \rvert = 1.25$
* **Market B (Leisure Tourists):** Price elasticity of demand $\lvert E_B \rvert = 2.0$

Calculate the ratio of prices ($P_A / P_B$) the airline should charge to maximize total profit.

#### 💡 Solution:
Using the Elasticity-Price Ratio Rule:
$$\dfrac{P_A}{P_B} = \dfrac{1 - \dfrac{1}{\lvert E_B \rvert}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{\lvert E_A \rvert}} = \dfrac{1 - \dfrac{1}{2.0}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{1.25}} = \dfrac{1 - 0.50}{1 - 0.80} = \dfrac{0.50}{0.20} = \mathbf{2.5}$$

**Interpretation:** The airline should charge business travelers in Market A a price **2.5 times higher** than leisure tourists in Market B.

---

### 📌 Question 4: Strategic Pricing Selection for a New Enterprise
**Problem:** A hospitality startup is opening two properties in Nepal:
1. A luxury wilderness boutique resort inside Chitwan National Park with private plunge pools and exclusive elephant safaris.
2. A budget chain of highway motels along the East-West Highway.

Recommend and justify the optimal new-product pricing strategy for each property.

#### 💡 Solution:
1. **Chitwan Luxury Boutique Resort $\implies$ Price Skimming Strategy:**
   * *Justification:* Unique differentiated product, high prestige appeal, price-inelastic high-income tourists ($\lvert E_d \rvert < 1$), high initial capital setup costs to recoup.
2. **Highway Budget Motels $\implies$ Penetration Pricing Strategy:**
   * *Justification:* Price-sensitive mass transit travelers ($\lvert E_d \rvert > 1$), intense competition from local lodges, requires high occupancy volume to achieve operational scale economies.

---

### 📌 Question 5: Optimal Transfer Pricing
**Problem:** An integrated conglomerate has an upstream paper mill producing pulp at marginal cost $\text{MC}_u = \text{Rs. } 40\text{ per kg}$. Its downstream publishing division uses the pulp to print textbooks with assembly marginal cost $\text{MC}_d = \text{Rs. } 20\text{ per book}$ (1 kg pulp per book).
1. What transfer price ($P_t$) should central management set if no external market exists for pulp?
2. What is the total marginal cost ($\text{MC}_{\text{Total}}$) for the publishing division?

#### 💡 Solution:
1. **Transfer Price ($P_t$):**  
   With no external market, to prevent double marginalization:
   $$P_t = \text{MC}_u = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 40\text{ per kg}}$$
2. **Total Marginal Cost ($\text{MC}_{\text{Total}}$):**
   $$\text{MC}_{\text{Total}} = P_t + \text{MC}_d = 40 + 20 = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 60\text{ per textbook}}$$

## 📌 Final Exam Takeaways
* **Cost-Plus ($P = \text{AVC}(1+m)$):** Practical, cost-recovering, but ignores demand.
* **Price Discrimination:** Feasible only when resale is prevented and sub-market elasticities differ ($P_{\text{Inelastic}} > P_{\text{Elastic}}$).
* **Peak-Load Pricing:** Rations capacity for non-storable services ($P_{\text{Peak}} > P_{\text{OffPeak}}$).
* **Transfer Pricing:** Set $P_t = \text{MC}_u$ to eliminate double marginalization.
* **Skimming vs. Penetration:** Skimming targets high margins first; Penetration targets high market share first.
