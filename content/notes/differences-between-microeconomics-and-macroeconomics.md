## 1. Historical Background: The Two Halves of Economics

In 1933, Norwegian economist and Nobel Laureate **Sir Ragnar Frisch** formally bifurcated the discipline of economics into two distinct branches: **Microeconomics** and **Macroeconomics**.

![differences between microeconomics and macroeconomics diagram 1](/flowcharts/introduction-to-economics/differences-between-microeconomics-and-macroeconomics-diagram-1.svg)

* **Micro** comes from the Greek word *'Mikros'* (meaning small).
* **Macro** comes from the Greek word *'Makros'* (meaning large).

---

## 2. What is Microeconomics? (Price Theory)

### Definition
**Microeconomics** is the branch of economics that studies the economic behavior and decision-making of **individual economic units** — such as an individual consumer, a single household, a specific business firm, or a single industry.

### Core Focus:
* How individual consumers allocate their limited income to maximize total satisfaction (Utility Maximization).
* How individual firms combine inputs to minimize costs and maximize profits.
* How the interaction of demand and supply determines the **price of a specific commodity** (e.g., price of tea, rice, smartphones, or hotel rooms).
* Why it is called **Price Theory**: Because its primary analytical tool is the determination of relative market prices for goods and factors of production.

### Examples of Microeconomic Topics:
* Demand and supply of automobiles.
* Pricing strategy of a local restaurant.
* Wage determination of software engineers in Kathmandu.
* Profit maximization for a hotel firm.

---

## 3. What is Macroeconomics? (Income and Employment Theory)

### Definition
**Macroeconomics** is the branch of economics that studies the **economy as a whole**. It examines nationwide aggregate variables and overall economic performance rather than individual parts.

### Core Focus:
* How the total level of national output and income (**Gross Domestic Product - GDP**) is determined.
* The causes and control of nationwide **inflation** (general rise in the price level) and **unemployment**.
* Long-term **economic growth**, business cycles (booms and recessions), and international balance of payments.
* Government economic policies:
  * **Monetary Policy:** Money supply, interest rates, and central banking (e.g., Nepal Rastra Bank policies).
  * **Fiscal Policy:** Government spending, taxation, budget deficits, and public debt.
* Why it is called **Income and Employment Theory**: Because its primary objective is explaining the determinants of aggregate national income and total employment.

### Examples of Macroeconomic Topics:
* Nepal's annual GDP growth rate.
* National consumer price inflation rate.
* Overall unemployment and foreign employment remittances.
* National government budget deficit and tax reform.

## 4. Seven Key Bases of Comparison: Micro vs. Macro

The foundational differences between Microeconomics and Macroeconomics are summarized across seven core dimensions:

* **1. Unit of Study:** Micro studies individual economic units (consumers, firms, specific markets); Macro studies the economy as an integrated whole (nationwide aggregates).
* **2. Alternative Name:** Micro is known as **Price Theory**; Macro is known as **Income and Employment Theory**.
* **3. Core Objective:** Micro focuses on optimal resource allocation by individual market agents; Macro focuses on full employment, price stability, and economic growth.
* **4. Price Variable Focus:** Micro explains the relative price of an individual commodity; Macro analyzes the general price level of the entire nation (inflation).
* **5. Output Focus:** Micro determines the output level of an individual firm or industry; Macro determines total aggregate national output (Real GDP).
* **6. Primary Analytical Tools:** Micro operates using individual Demand and individual Supply; Macro operates using Aggregate Demand ($AD$) and Aggregate Supply ($AS$).
* **7. Policy Formulation:** Micro guides market regulation, price ceilings, and fair competition; Macro drives Monetary policy (interest rates) and Fiscal policy (government budgets).

## 6. Interdependence Between Micro and Macroeconomics

Microeconomics and macroeconomics are not mutually exclusive; they are **interdependent and complementary halves of the same subject**:

### 1. Microeconomics depends on Macroeconomics:
* A business firm (micro unit) cannot plan its sales without considering national inflation, GDP growth rates, and interest rates set by the central bank (macro conditions).
* If national unemployment rises and purchasing power falls (macro), sales for individual consumer shops will decline (micro).

### 2. Macroeconomics is built on Microeconomic Foundations:
* National Aggregate Demand (macro) is simply the sum of the consumption choices of millions of individual households (micro).
* Total national output (GDP) is the aggregate production of all individual firms in the country.

## 7. The Fallacy of Composition & Economic Paradoxes

What is true for an individual (micro) is **not necessarily true** for the economy as a whole (macro). This is known as the **Fallacy of Composition**.

### Classic Example: The Paradox of Thrift
* **At the Micro level:** If an individual person saves more money, they increase their personal wealth and future financial security (prudent behavior).
* **At the Macro level:** If *every citizen simultaneously* decides to save more and drastically cut consumption expenditure, total business revenues plummet $\rightarrow$ firms lay off workers $\rightarrow$ national income collapses $\rightarrow$ overall total savings in the economy paradoxically fall.
