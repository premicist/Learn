In 1933, Norwegian economist **Sir Ragnar Frisch** bifurcated economics into Microeconomics and Macroeconomics. Both branches analyze resource allocation at different levels of aggregation.

| **Basis of Difference**      | **Microeconomics**                                                                                             | **Macroeconomics**                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Origin & Etymology**    | Derived from Greek 'Mikros' (small)                                                                            | Derived from Greek 'Makros' (large)                                                                               |
| **2. Meaning & Scope**       | Studies individual units (a consumer, firm, or industry)                                                       | Studies the economy as a whole and national aggregates                                                            |
| **3. Alternative Name**      | Known as 'Price Theory'                                                                                        | Known as 'Income & Employment Theory'                                                                             |
| **4. Core Subject Matter**   | Individual demand, supply, product pricing, & factor pricing                                                   | National Income, GDP, General Price Level, & Inflation                                                            |
| **5. Key Assumptions**       | Assumes 'Ceteris Paribus' & full employment of resources                                                       | Does not assume full employment; analyzes economic slumps                                                         |
| **6. Analytical Method**     | Uses Partial Equilibrium Analysis (single market in isolation)                                                 | Uses General / Macroeconomic Equilibrium Analysis                                                                 |
| **7. Boulding's Metaphor**   | Prof. Boulding: 'Study of individual trees, not the forest'                                                    | Prof. Boulding: 'Study of the entire forest, not trees'                                                           |
| **8. Perspective / View**    | Provides a 'Worm's-eye view' of individual units                                                               | Provides a 'Bird's-eye view' of the macro economy                                                                 |
| **9. Equilibrium Condition** | Individual Demand = Individual Supply (d = s)                                                                  | Aggregate Demand = Aggregate Supply (AD = AS)                                                                     |
| **10. Concrete Examples**    | • Demand for hotel rooms in Pokhara<br>• Price of a specific commodity<br>• Profit of an individual hotel firm | • Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of Nepal<br>• National Inflation Rate<br>• Overall Unemployment Rate in an economy |

### Interdependence & Economic Paradoxes

* **Complementary Nature:** Micro and macroeconomics are interdependent. Aggregate national demand is the sum of individual household demands.
* **Paradox of Thrift:** What is beneficial at the micro level may be harmful at the macro level. Individual saving increases personal security, but universal aggregate saving reduces total national expenditure, output, and income.
