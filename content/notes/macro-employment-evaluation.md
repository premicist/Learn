## Different adjustment stories

The classical approach relies more heavily on price and wage flexibility. The Keynesian approach gives greater weight to aggregate demand, expectations, liquidity, and short-run rigidities.

## Policy implications

Classical reasoning may favour limited intervention when markets adjust effectively. Keynesian reasoning supports demand management when unemployment and idle capacity persist.

## Model limitations

Both approaches simplify complex economies. Real outcomes depend on institutions, expectations, supply constraints, financial conditions, distribution, and the time horizon.
