## 1. What is the Total Price Effect?

When the price of Good X falls while consumer income ($M$) and the price of Good Y ($P_y$) remain constant, two things happen simultaneously:

1. **Good X becomes relatively cheaper** than Good Y (Relative Price Change).
2. **The consumer's real purchasing power rises**, because less money is needed to buy the original basket of goods (Real Income Change).

According to modern microeconomics, the **Total Price Effect** is the combined result of these two distinct underlying forces:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│       TOTAL PRICE EFFECT = SUBSTITUTION EFFECT + INCOME EFFECT          │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## 2. The Substitution Effect

### Definition
The **Substitution Effect** measures the change in the quantity demanded of Good X resulting purely from the change in its **relative price**, holding the consumer's **real income (utility level) constant**.

* **Direction:** The substitution effect is **always negative** (inversely related to price). When Good X becomes cheaper, a rational consumer always substitutes more of Good X in place of Good Y ($P_x \downarrow \implies Q_x \uparrow$).
* **Graphical Representation:** The consumer moves along the **same indifference curve ($IC_1$)** to a new tangency point with a flatter price slope.

---

## 3. The Income Effect

### Definition
The **Income Effect** measures the change in quantity demanded resulting solely from the change in the consumer's **real purchasing power** caused by the price reduction, holding relative prices constant.

The direction of the income effect depends on the nature of the commodity:

1. **For Normal Goods:** Real income rises $\implies$ Quantity demanded of X increases ($P_x \downarrow \implies Q_x \uparrow$). The income effect reinforces the substitution effect.
2. **For Inferior Goods:** Real income rises $\implies$ Quantity demanded of X decreases ($P_x \downarrow \implies Q_x \downarrow$). The income effect opposes the substitution effect.
3. **For Giffen Goods (Extreme Inferior):** The negative income effect is **so powerful** that it completely overwhelms the substitution effect, causing total quantity demanded to fall when price drops ($P_x \downarrow \implies Q_x \downarrow$).

---

## 4. Methods of Price Effect Decomposition

Economists use two primary analytical methods to separate the substitution effect from the income effect:

![income price and substitution effects diagram 1](/flowcharts/introduction-to-economics/income-price-and-substitution-effects-diagram-1.svg)

### 1. Hicksian Compensating Variation Approach (J.R. Hicks)
* After the price of Good X falls, the consumer's nominal income is hypothetically reduced (taxed away) just enough to bring them back to their **original indifference curve ($IC_1$)**.
* The movement along $IC_1$ isolates the pure **Substitution Effect**.
* Restoring the consumer's purchasing power causes a parallel outward shift to $IC_2$, isolating the **Income Effect**.

### 2. Slutsky's Approach (Eugene Slutsky)
* Money income is adjusted by the exact cost difference needed to allow the consumer to purchase the **original physical bundle of goods**.
* Slutsky's substitution effect moves the consumer to a slightly higher indifference curve ($IC'$), making it empirically testable with market data.

## 5. Summary Matrix: Price Effect Across Commodity Types

| Commodity Category | Substitution Effect ($P_x \downarrow$) | Income Effect ($P_x \downarrow$) | Net Price Effect ($P_x \downarrow$) | Law of Demand Holds? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal Goods** | Positive (Buys more X) | Positive (Buys more X) | **Strongly Positive ($Q_x \uparrow$)** | **Yes (Standard DD)** |
| **Inferior Goods (Non-Giffen)** | Positive (Buys more X) | Negative (Buys less X) | **Positive ($Q_x \uparrow$)** (Sub Effect > Income Effect) | **Yes (Downward DD)** |
| **Giffen Goods** | Positive (Buys more X) | Strongly Negative (Buys less X) | **Negative ($Q_x \downarrow$)** (Income Effect > Sub Effect) | **No (Upward DD)** |

## 6. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **State the Price Effect equation.**  
   *Answer:* $\text{Total Price Effect} = \text{Substitution Effect} + \text{Income Effect}$.
2. **What is the sign of the Substitution Effect?**  
   *Answer:* Always negative (a price drop always induces substitution toward the cheaper good).
3. **Why do Giffen goods violate the Law of Demand?**  
   *Answer:* Because the negative income effect is larger than the substitution effect, causing demand to fall when price drops.

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Explain how the total price effect is decomposed into substitution and income effects using Hicks's compensating variation method.
2. Differentiate between normal goods, inferior goods, and Giffen goods based on the direction of their income effect.
