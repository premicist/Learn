## Imperfect competition

Imperfect competition exists when sellers have some control over price. Product differentiation, barriers to entry, limited information, or a small number of firms can give a firm market power.

## Monopoly

A monopoly has one dominant seller and strong barriers to entry. The firm faces the market demand curve, which is downward sloping. To sell more units, it generally needs to reduce price, so marginal revenue lies below average revenue.

## Monopoly equilibrium

A monopolist chooses the output where marginal revenue equals marginal cost and then reads the price from the demand or average-revenue curve at that output. This can produce a price above marginal cost and a positive economic profit when demand and cost conditions allow it.

## Comparing market outcomes

Compared with the competitive benchmark, a monopoly may produce less and charge a higher price. The result can create a transfer of surplus and a deadweight loss. The size of the effect depends on demand, cost, regulation, and the strength of entry barriers.

## Other market structures

Monopolistic competition combines many sellers with differentiated products. Oligopoly has a small number of interdependent firms. In both cases, the decisions of one firm can influence the choices of others.

## Policy and evaluation

Competition policy, price regulation, public ownership, and consumer protection can address some problems associated with market power. A strong answer should discuss both the possible efficiency problem and the practical limits of intervention.
