## Overview

Goods and services cannot be produced by a single input in isolation. Production requires combining the four fundamental **Factors of Production**: **Land**, **Labour**, **Capital**, and **Organisation (Entrepreneurship)**. The study of how the total income generated by these factors is distributed among them as factor rewards is called **Factor Pricing** or the **Theory of Distribution**.

## Part 1: Factors of Production and Factor Pricing

### Q1. What is Factor Pricing (Theory of Distribution)?
**Answer:**
**Definition:**
> **Factor Pricing** (also known as the **Theory of Functional Distribution**) is the branch of microeconomics that explains how the prices of the factors of production—**Rent for Land, Wages for Labour, Interest for Capital, and Profit for Organisation**—are determined in the factor markets.

#### Functional vs. Personal Distribution:
* **Functional Distribution:** Explains how total national income is divided among the four productive factors according to their economic contribution to production (e.g., share of wages, share of rent).
* **Personal Distribution:** Explains how total national income is distributed among individual persons and households in a country, regardless of which factor they provide.

### Q2. Explain the four Factors of Production, their characteristics, and their factor rewards.
**Answer:**

![class12 factors of production concepts diagram 2](/flowcharts/class-12/class12-factors-of-production-concepts-diagram-2.svg)

#### 1. Land:
* **Meaning:** All natural resources and gifts of nature on, above, or beneath the earth's surface (agricultural soil, minerals, rivers, forests, sunlight).
* **Key Characteristics:** Free gift of nature, strictly fixed in total supply, completely immobile, and indestructible in its basic physical existence.
* **Factor Reward:** **Rent**.

#### 2. Labour:
* **Meaning:** Any physical or mental human exertion undertaken with the expectation of earning an economic income.
* **Key Characteristics:** Inseparable from the labourer, highly perishable (cannot be stored for tomorrow), weak bargaining power compared to capital, and variable in efficiency.
* **Factor Reward:** **Wages / Salaries**.

#### 3. Capital:
* **Meaning:** All man-made durable assets, tools, machines, transport vehicles, factories, and technology used to produce further goods and services.
* **Key Characteristics:** Produced by human effort (saving and investment), mobile, subject to physical wear and tear (depreciation), and increases labour productivity.
* **Factor Reward:** **Interest**.

#### 4. Organisation / Entrepreneurship:
* **Meaning:** The human capability and enterprise that combines land, labour, and capital, takes business decisions, introduces innovations, and bears financial risks and market uncertainties.
* **Key Characteristics:** Non-transferable leadership, decision-maker, bearer of uninsurable business risk, and recipient of the residual earnings.
* **Factor Reward:** **Profit** (which can be positive or negative/loss).

## Part 2: Product Pricing vs. Factor Pricing

---

### Q3. How does Factor Pricing differ from Commodity (Product) Pricing?
**Answer:**

| Comparison Basis | Commodity (Product) Pricing | Factor (Resource) Pricing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature of Demand** | **Direct Demand:** Consumers demand goods directly to satisfy their immediate personal wants. | **Derived Demand:** Factors are demanded not for themselves, but derived from the demand for the final goods they produce. |
| **Nature of Supply** | Determined by the direct cost of production of that physical commodity. | Determined by demographic factors (for labour), natural endowments (for land), and savings (for capital). |
| **Joint Demand** | Goods can often be bought and consumed independently. | **Joint Demand:** Factors must be used together; land alone or labour alone cannot produce anything. |
| **Price Determination** | Determined by interaction of product demand and supply. | Determined by Marginal Productivity and factor market demand and supply. |

## Quick Revision Check

1. **What is Factor Pricing?**  
   *The economic process of determining the rewards paid to the factors of production: Rent, Wages, Interest, and Profit.*
2. **Name the four factors of production and their respective factor rewards.**  
   *Land (Rent), Labour (Wages), Capital (Interest), and Organisation (Profit).*
3. **Why is the demand for a factor of production called 'Derived Demand'?**  
   *Because factors are not demanded for their own sake, but for the final goods and services they help produce.*
4. **Which factor of production earns a residual reward that can be negative?**  
   *Organisation / Entrepreneurship (Profit).*
