### Meaning

The Risk-Bearing Theory of Profit was propounded by Professor F. B. Hawley in 1907. According to him:

> “Profit is the reward for taking risks and responsibilities in business.”

According to this theory, profit is related to the risk borne by the entrepreneur. Generally, higher risk may lead to higher potential profit, while lower risk may lead to lower potential profit. However, risk does not guarantee profit; a risky business can also suffer losses.

According to Hawley, an entrepreneur faces four types of risk:

1. **Replacement risk:** Also known as depreciation risk. It can generally be calculated and is included in the cost of production.

2. **Obsolescence risk:** This arises when machinery, technology, products, or methods become outdated because of technological progress or changes in consumer preferences. It is difficult to estimate accurately, although it may be included in business costs.

3. **Risk proper:** This arises from the time gap between producing goods and selling them. During this period, prices, demand, competition, or market conditions may change unexpectedly.

4. **Uncertainty:** This refers to unpredictable events whose outcomes or probabilities cannot be calculated accurately. For example:sudden changes in government policy, consumer tastes, economic conditions, or market demand.

The other factors (land, labour and capital) are only the means of production and remain unutilized in the absence of entrepreneur. Hence, the entrepreneur must get reward for risk taking function as profit.


### **Criticism of the Risk Bearing Theory of Profit**

The major criticisms of the theory are:

i. Profit arises not because of bearing risk (as claimed by the theory) but because of efficient management and coordination of factors by the entrepreneur.

ii. Profit is not the reward for bearing risk (as claimed by the theory) but rather the reward for avoiding and reducing risk in business by efficient entrepreneurs.

iii. There is no direct relationship between risk and profit (as claimed by the theory).

iv. Profit does not arise from all kinds of risk (as claimed by the theory). It arises only from unforeseeable risks that cannot be insured, such as changes in taste, fashion, habits of people, new discoveries, etc. It does not arise from foreseeable (insurable) risks such as theft, fire, or accidents, which are not true risks in the economic sense.
