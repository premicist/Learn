## Meaning and nature of social change

Social change is a significant transformation in social relationships, institutions, culture, or patterns of behaviour over time. It may be gradual or rapid, planned or unplanned, and beneficial for some groups while creating difficulties for others.

## Drivers of change

Modernisation, diffusion, technology, social media, migration, globalisation, economic change, and environmental pressures can alter how people work, communicate, consume, and organise. These drivers often interact rather than operating separately.

## Social movements

A social movement is a sustained collective effort to promote or resist change. Movements may focus on rights, identity, labour, the environment, public services, or other shared concerns. Their methods can include awareness-building, organising, negotiation, protest, and participation in public debate.

## Effects on the workforce

Social change can alter skills, occupations, employment relationships, workplace expectations, and access to opportunity. Technology may create new jobs while reducing demand for some tasks, making reskilling and fair transition important.

## Effects on markets and business

Changing values and lifestyles influence products, services, branding, recruitment, and customer expectations. Organisations that monitor social change can adapt more effectively, but they should avoid treating diverse communities as a single uniform market.
