## Meaning and scope of demography

Demography is the study of human populations, including their size, composition, distribution, and change. Births, deaths, migration, age, sex, education, occupation, and household patterns are common areas of demographic analysis.

## Distribution and density

Population distribution describes where people live, while population density describes the number of people relative to an area. Physical geography, employment, infrastructure, services, history, security, and public policy can influence both. High density is not automatically a problem, and low density is not automatically an advantage.

## Population composition

Population can be classified into urban and rural groups, working and dependent groups, or people above and below a poverty line. Age, sex, education, and occupation profiles help organisations anticipate labour supply, consumer demand, service needs, and training requirements.

## Tools of demographic analysis

A census aims to count and describe the population at a particular time. Surveys collect information from a sample, while vital statistics record events such as births, deaths, and marriages. Each tool has strengths and limitations, so the definition, coverage, and date should be checked before comparison.

## Population as a resource

People contribute knowledge, labour, creativity, care, entrepreneurship, and demand. Investment in education, health, skills, safety, and decent work can strengthen human resources and support productive growth.

## Demographic change and business

Ageing, youth unemployment, migration, urban growth, and changing household patterns can affect recruitment, skills, product design, location, and long-term strategy. A demographic dividend is possible when a large working-age population is healthy, skilled, and productively employed.

## Inequality and responsible analysis

Demographic averages can hide differences across gender, place, class, ethnicity, disability, and other social positions. Business decisions should consider unequal access to opportunities and the social effects of recruitment, services, and investment.
