## Meaning

Price discrimination occurs when a seller charges different prices for related units or customer groups and the difference is not explained only by cost differences.

## Conditions

The seller needs some market power, the ability to identify or separate customer groups, and limited resale between groups. Differences in willingness to pay are important.

## Forms

First-degree discrimination aims to charge each buyer near their maximum willingness to pay. Second-degree discrimination varies price with quantity or package. Third-degree discrimination charges different groups different prices.

## Evaluation

Discrimination can expand output in some settings and cover fixed costs, but it can also transfer surplus and raise fairness concerns. The result depends on the market and the allocation of output.
