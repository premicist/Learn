## Sustainable development

Sustainable development seeks to improve wellbeing today without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their needs. It requires economic progress to be considered alongside social inclusion and environmental protection.

## The SDG framework

The Sustainable Development Goals organise global development priorities into measurable areas such as poverty, health, education, gender equality, decent work, reduced inequality, climate action, and strong institutions. The framework helps structure discussion, but indicators must still be interpreted carefully.

## Applying the goals to Nepal

A Nepal-focused answer can connect a local issue to one or more goals. For example, reliable electricity may support education, health services, enterprise, employment, and cleaner production. The analysis should identify both the intended benefit and the conditions needed for delivery.

## Measuring progress

Indicators can be input, output, outcome, or impact measures. Building a school is an input or output achievement; improved learning is an outcome. Separating these levels prevents implementation from being confused with final development impact.

## Trade-offs and coordination

A project may improve one indicator while placing pressure on another. Sustainable planning therefore requires coordination, transparent financing, local participation, and attention to environmental limits.
