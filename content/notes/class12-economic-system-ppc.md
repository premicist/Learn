## The central allocation problem

An economy has limited resources and many competing wants. It must decide what to produce, how to produce, and for whom to produce. These choices are connected to scarcity, opportunity cost, and the need to allocate resources efficiently.

## Economic systems

A market economy relies mainly on prices and private decisions. A command economy relies more heavily on public planning and administrative decisions. A mixed economy combines market signals with government action. In practice, every system faces trade-offs between efficiency, equity, stability, and freedom of choice.

## Production possibility curve

A production possibility curve shows the maximum combinations of two goods that can be produced with given resources and technology. A point on the curve represents productive efficiency, a point inside it shows underuse of resources, and a point outside it is unattainable with the current conditions.

## Opportunity cost and the curve

Moving along the curve requires producing more of one good by giving up some of the other. The slope represents opportunity cost. A bowed-out curve can show increasing opportunity cost when resources are not equally suited to producing both goods.

## Growth and a shift outward

An increase in resources, an improvement in technology, or better organisation can shift the curve outward. The shift represents greater productive capacity, not an automatic guarantee that every person will benefit equally.
