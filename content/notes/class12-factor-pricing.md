## Distribution and factor rewards

Factor pricing studies how income is distributed among the factors of production. Land receives rent, labour receives wages, capital receives interest, and organisation or entrepreneurship is associated with profit.

## Rent and scarcity

Rent is a payment for the use of land or other natural resources. Economic rent is the part of a factor’s payment that exceeds the minimum needed to keep it in its current use. Scarcity, quality, location, and alternative uses influence rent.

For the detailed rent discussion, use the existing resource **Factor Pricing: Rent and the Ricardian Theory** in this unit.

## Wages

Wages are payments for labour services. Labour demand is derived from the demand for the goods and services labour helps produce. Skills, productivity, working conditions, bargaining power, and labour-market institutions can affect wage differences.

## Interest

Interest is the return to lending or using financial capital over time. A simple explanation should distinguish the nominal interest rate from the real interest rate and consider the role of time preference, risk, and expected inflation.

## Profit

Profit is the return that remains after a firm pays its explicit and implicit costs. It can reward innovation, organisation, risk-bearing, and successful coordination, while competition may reduce abnormal profit over time.
