## Market demand and the firm

Market demand is the total quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices. A firm faces the demand conditions relevant to its market. The shape of that demand relationship affects the price it can charge and the revenue it receives.

## Total revenue

Total revenue is the money received from sales:

**TR = Price × Quantity sold**

A revenue table helps organise price, quantity, and total revenue before drawing any curve. When price changes, total revenue may rise, fall, or remain unchanged depending on the response of quantity demanded.

## Average revenue

Average revenue is revenue per unit sold:

**AR = TR ÷ Quantity = Price**

For a firm selling at one market price, the average revenue curve is the firm’s demand curve. Under different market conditions, the curve may be horizontal or downward sloping.

## Marginal revenue

Marginal revenue is the change in total revenue from selling one additional unit:

**MR = Change in TR ÷ Change in Quantity**

When a seller must lower price to sell more units, marginal revenue can lie below average revenue because the lower price applies to earlier units as well.

## Reading a revenue diagram

Label the axes, identify the demand or average-revenue curve, and show the marginal-revenue relationship. Always state whether the market is being treated as perfectly competitive or imperfectly competitive before interpreting the diagram.

## Quick check

Prepare a small table with prices 10, 8, 6, and 4 and quantities 1, 2, 3, and 4. Calculate total revenue and the marginal revenue between each quantity.
