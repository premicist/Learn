## 1. What is an Indifference Curve?

An **Indifference Curve (IC)** is the geometric locus of all commodity combinations of two goods (say, Good $X$ and Good $Y$) that yield the **exact same level of total utility or satisfaction** to the consumer.

Because every combination on the curve provides identical satisfaction, the consumer has no preference for one combination over another and is completely **indifferent** among them. Hence, an indifference curve is also known as an **Iso-Utility Curve** or **Equal Utility Curve**.

$$U = f(X, Y) = \text{Constant } (k)$$

Along an indifference curve, the total change in utility is zero ($dU = 0$):

$$dU = \left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial X}\right) dX + \left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial Y}\right) dY = 0$$

$$-\dfrac{dY}{dX} = \dfrac{MU_x}{MU_y} = MRS_{xy}$$

> **Prof. J.R. Hicks's Definition:** *"An indifference curve is the locus of points representing different combinations of two goods which yield equal satisfaction to the consumer, so that he is indifferent as to which combination he consumes."*

## 2. The Indifference Schedule

An **indifference schedule** is a numerical table listing different combinations of two goods that provide the exact same total satisfaction to a consumer:

| Combination Bundle | Quantity of Good X (Units) | Quantity of Good Y (Units) | Rate of Sacrifice ($\Delta Y / \Delta X$) | Total Utility Level |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **A** | 1 | 12 | — | $U_1$ |
| **B** | 2 | 8 | 4 Units of Y for 1 X | $U_1$ |
| **C** | 3 | 5 | 3 Units of Y for 1 X | $U_1$ |
| **D** | 4 | 3 | 2 Units of Y for 1 X | $U_1$ |
| **E** | 5 | 2 | 1 Unit of Y for 1 X | $U_1$ |

### Interpretation of the Schedule:
* At combination **A**, the consumer has 1 unit of X and 12 units of Y.
* Moving to combination **B**, to acquire 1 extra unit of X ($\Delta X = +1$), the consumer gives up 4 units of Y ($\Delta Y = -4$).
* Moving to **C**, the consumer sacrifices only 3 units of Y for another unit of X.
* All combinations (A, B, C, D, E) give the consumer identical total satisfaction ($U_1$).

## 3. The Indifference Map

An **Indifference Map** is a complete family or set of indifference curves drawn on a single two-dimensional graph.

![Indifference Curve and Indifference Map](/images/uploads/indifference-curve-and-map.svg)

### Key Characteristics of the Indifference Map:
1. **Complete Preference Representation:** An indifference map graphically portrays the consumer's entire scale of preferences across all possible commodity bundles.
2. **Higher Curve Represents Higher Satisfaction:** An indifference curve positioned higher and to the right ($IC_3 > IC_2 > IC_1$) represents a **higher level of total utility** because it contains larger quantities of either Good X, Good Y, or both (Axiom of Non-Satiety).
3. **Infinite Curves:** Between any two indifference curves, an infinite number of intermediate curves can be drawn representing intermediate satisfaction levels.

## 4. Review & Exam Practice Questions

### Very Short Questions (1-2 Marks):
1. **Define an Indifference Curve.**  
   *Answer:* The geometric curve showing all combinations of two goods that provide equal total satisfaction to the consumer.
2. **What is an Indifference Map?**  
   *Answer:* A family or set of indifference curves plotted on a single graph showing a consumer's complete scale of preferences.
3. **Why does a higher indifference curve represent higher satisfaction?**  
   *Answer:* Because under monotonic preferences (non-satiety), higher curves contain larger quantities of goods.

### Short Questions (3-5 Marks):
1. Explain the concept of an Indifference Schedule and construct an Indifference Curve from it.
2. Explain the significance of an Indifference Map in analyzing consumer preferences.
