## Overview

The concept of **Equilibrium** is the central unifying principle in microeconomic theory. Originating from physics, equilibrium describes a state of balance where opposing economic forces (such as demand and supply, or revenue and cost) are equalized, so that there is no internal tendency to change. This lesson covers the concept of equilibrium, the distinction between a firm and an industry, and the various classifications of economic equilibrium.

## Part 1: Meaning of Equilibrium and Market Balance

### Q1. What does 'Equilibrium' mean in economics?
**Answer:**
**Definition:**
> In economics, **Equilibrium** refers to a state of balance or rest in which opposing economic forces (such as buyers' demand and sellers' supply, or marginal revenue and marginal cost) perfectly balance each other, leaving no individual, firm, or market with any incentive or tendency to change their current behavior or position.
> *Linguistic Origin:* Derived from Latin words **'Aequus'** (equal) and **'Libra'** (balance / weight).

#### Everyday Analogy:
Think of a pair of weighing scales: when equal weights are placed on both pans, the pointer stays perfectly centered at rest. Similarly, when the quantity demanded by buyers at a given price equals the quantity supplied by sellers, the market is in equilibrium.

## Part 2: Market Equilibrium (Demand and Supply)

### Q3. How is Market Equilibrium determined? Explain with a schedule and diagram.
**Answer:**
Market equilibrium is established at the price where **Market Demand ($Q_d$) equals Market Supply ($Q_s$)**.

#### Market Schedule:

| Price ($P$) [Rs.] | Quantity Demanded ($Q_d$) [Units] | Quantity Supplied ($Q_s$) [Units] | Market Condition | Pressure on Price |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $50$ | $100$ | $500$ | Excess Supply ($+400$) | Price falls $\downarrow$ |
| $40$ | $200$ | $400$ | Excess Supply ($+200$) | Price falls $\downarrow$ |
| **30** | **300** | **300** | **Equilibrium ($Q_d = Q_s$)** | **Stable / At Rest** |
| $20$ | $400$ | $200$ | Excess Demand ($-200$) | Price rises $\uparrow$ |
| $10$ | $500$ | $100$ | Excess Demand ($-400$) | Price rises $\uparrow$ |

![Market Equilibrium Price Determination (Demand and Supply)](/images/uploads/market-equilibrium-price-determination.svg)

#### Explanation:
1. **At Price Rs. 30 (Point E):** $Q_d = Q_s = 300 \text{ units}$. There is neither shortage nor surplus. The market clears at equilibrium price **Rs. 30**.
2. **Above Rs. 30 (Excess Supply):** Sellers cannot sell all their goods, creating unsold inventory. Competition among sellers forces the price down to Rs. 30.
3. **Below Rs. 30 (Excess Demand):** Buyers cannot buy all the goods they want at low prices. Competition among buyers bids the price up to Rs. 30.

### Q4. Distinguish between Partial Equilibrium and General Equilibrium.
**Answer:**

![class12 price output concept of equilibrium diagram 3](/flowcharts/class-12/class12-price-output-concept-of-equilibrium-diagram-3.svg)

| Basis | Partial Equilibrium | General Equilibrium |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pioneering Economist** | **Alfred Marshall** (1890). | **Léon Walras** (1874). |
| **Scope of Analysis** | Analyzes the equilibrium of a **single market, consumer, firm, or industry in isolation**. | Analyzes the simultaneous equilibrium of **all markets, prices, and factors across the entire economy**. |
| **Core Assumption** | Relies on the *Ceteris Paribus* assumption ("all other factors remain constant"). | Recognizes that a price change in one market causes chain reactions in other markets. |
| **Simplicity** | Simple, practical, and easy to apply for microeconomic decision-making. | Mathematically complex, multi-equation system. |
| **Example** | Analyzing how an increase in the price of tea affects tea consumption, assuming coffee prices remain unchanged. | Analyzing how an increase in petroleum prices impacts transport costs, food inflation, wage demands, and exchange rates. |

## Quick Revision Check

1. **What is the literal meaning of Equilibrium?**  
   *A state of balance where opposing forces are equal and there is no tendency to change.*
2. **What is the difference between a firm and an industry?**  
   *A firm is an individual producing unit, while an industry is the aggregate collection of all firms producing identical or related products.*
3. **What happens in a market when the price is set above the equilibrium price?**  
   *Excess supply (surplus) occurs, forcing sellers to lower prices back to equilibrium.*
