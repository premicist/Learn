## Market equilibrium

Market equilibrium occurs where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. The equilibrium price and quantity can be found from a table, equations, or the intersection of demand and supply curves.

## Changes in equilibrium

A demand shift changes the equilibrium through buyer-side conditions, while a supply shift changes it through producer-side conditions. The direction of the price and quantity change depends on which curve moves and by how much.

## Taxes and subsidies

A tax creates a wedge between the price paid by buyers and the price received by sellers. A subsidy lowers an effective cost or increases a payment, encouraging supply or demand according to its design.

## Price controls

A binding price ceiling can create a shortage, while a binding price floor can create a surplus. Non-binding controls do not change the market equilibrium.

## Efficiency

Economic surplus compares benefits and costs across market participants. Policy evaluation should consider efficiency, distribution, administration, and unintended effects.
