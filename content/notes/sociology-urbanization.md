## Meaning of urbanisation

Urbanisation is the growth of urban populations and the expansion of urban ways of living. It involves more than movement from rural to urban areas; it also includes changes in occupations, institutions, services, built environments, and social behaviour.

## Three ways to understand it

As a demographic concept, urbanisation refers to changes in the size and share of people living in urban places. As a structural concept, it involves changes in occupations, infrastructure, institutions, and land use. As a behavioural concept, it includes changing lifestyles, aspirations, consumption, and interaction.

## Causes and patterns

Employment, education, health services, transport, communication, housing, safety, and social networks can attract people to urban areas. Urban growth may be planned or unplanned and can produce very different experiences across neighbourhoods and groups.

## Infrastructure and labour

Urbanisation increases demand for housing, water, sanitation, energy, transport, education, health, and digital connectivity. It can widen access to jobs and services, but congestion, insecure work, high living costs, pollution, and unequal access can also intensify.

## Markets and business opportunities

Growing urban populations can create demand for retail, housing, mobility, food services, education, health, finance, entertainment, and digital platforms. Successful services must understand neighbourhood differences and respond to affordability, accessibility, and trust.

## Responsible urban enterprise

Businesses can contribute positively through inclusive design, safer work, resource-efficient operations, and partnerships with communities. Urban opportunities should be evaluated alongside external costs such as congestion, displacement, waste, and environmental pressure.
