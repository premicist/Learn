## What money does

Money is anything generally accepted as a medium of exchange. It also serves as a unit of account, a store of value, and a standard of deferred payment. These functions reduce the difficulties of barter and make prices easier to compare.

Good money is portable, durable, divisible, recognisable, and relatively stable in value. In modern economies, commercial banks and digital payment systems also support the payments process.

## Narrow and broad money

Narrow money generally includes the most liquid forms of money used directly for transactions, such as currency and some demand deposits. Broad money includes narrow money plus less-liquid deposits and related balances. The exact statistical definition can vary, so state the measure being used before interpreting a figure.

## Quantity theory intuition

The quantity theory connects the money supply, the speed at which money circulates, the average price level, and real output. In its simplest form:

\[
MV = PY
\]

Here, \(M\) is money supply, \(V\) is velocity, \(P\) is the price level, and \(Y\) is real output. The identity is a framework for analysis; a change in money does not automatically produce the same proportional change in prices when velocity or output also changes.

## Inflation and deflation

**Inflation** is a sustained rise in the general price level. It lowers the purchasing power of money when incomes do not rise as quickly as prices. **Deflation** is a sustained fall in the general price level and can discourage spending and investment when households and firms expect prices to keep falling.

Demand-pull inflation can arise when total spending grows faster than productive capacity. Cost-push inflation can arise when input costs, imported goods, or supply disruptions increase production costs.

## Effects and responses

Inflation affects households, savers, borrowers, firms, and people receiving fixed incomes differently. It can reduce real wages and savings while lowering the real burden of fixed nominal debt. A policy response should be judged by its effect on both price stability and output or employment.

## Practice prompt

Suppose nominal income rises by 5 percent while the general price level rises by 8 percent. Explain what happens to real purchasing power, assuming other conditions remain unchanged.
