## Fiscal expansion

Higher government spending or lower taxes can shift the IS schedule to the right. Output rises, but the resulting interest-rate increase may reduce some private spending through crowding out.

## Monetary expansion

A higher real money supply can shift the LM schedule to the right or downward. Interest rates tend to fall and output rises when spending responds to financing conditions.

## Relative efficacy

The strength of either policy depends on the slopes of IS and LM, interest sensitivity, expectations, liquidity conditions, spare capacity, and the time horizon.
