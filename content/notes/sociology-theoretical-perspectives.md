## Why perspectives matter

A sociological perspective is a way of selecting questions, concepts, and evidence. No single perspective explains every social situation, so comparing lenses can reveal different causes, experiences, and consequences.

## Structural functionalism

Structural functionalism views society as a system of connected parts. Institutions and norms can contribute to order, continuity, and coordination. A criticism is that the perspective may understate conflict, unequal power, and the possibility that institutions can benefit some groups more than others.

## Conflict theory

Conflict theory focuses on power, scarce resources, competing interests, and inequality. It asks who benefits from an arrangement and whose voice is missing. A criticism is that an emphasis on conflict may understate cooperation, shared values, and the stabilising functions of institutions.

## Symbolic interactionism

Symbolic interactionism examines meaning created through everyday interaction. Labels, language, gestures, and workplace routines influence how people interpret situations and present themselves. A criticism is that a close focus on small interactions may overlook larger institutions and historical forces.

## Postmodernism

Postmodern approaches question the idea that one universal explanation can describe every social experience. They attend to diversity, changing identities, media, language, and fragmented forms of power. A criticism is that broad scepticism can make it difficult to identify shared patterns or evaluate explanations consistently.

## Using theory in management

A manager can use a functionalist lens to examine coordination, a conflict lens to investigate unequal outcomes, an interactionist lens to improve communication, and a postmodern lens to recognise diverse identities and changing meanings.

## Quick check

Describe one workplace problem through two different perspectives. Explain what each perspective notices and what it may leave out.
