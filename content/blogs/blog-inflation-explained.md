## What inflation means

Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level. It does not mean that every price rises at the same rate, nor does it mean that a single product becoming more expensive is enough to establish economy-wide inflation.

## Demand-pull and cost-push causes

**Demand-pull inflation** occurs when total spending grows faster than the economy’s ability to produce goods and services. **Cost-push inflation** occurs when production costs—such as energy, imported inputs, or wages—rise and firms pass some of those costs on to buyers.

Expectations can also matter. If households and firms expect prices to keep rising, they may bring purchases forward, negotiate higher wages, or raise prices in advance, which can reinforce inflationary pressure.

## How inflation affects households

Inflation reduces the purchasing power of money when incomes do not rise as quickly as prices. Its effects are uneven: borrowers may benefit from repaying fixed nominal debts with less valuable money, while savers and people with fixed incomes may lose purchasing power.

## Why measurement matters

Economists commonly track inflation with a price index that compares the cost of a representative basket over time. The headline rate can be complemented by measures that exclude especially volatile categories, but every measure should be interpreted according to what it includes and how the basket is constructed.

Understanding both the cause and the distributional effect of inflation is essential before evaluating a policy response.
