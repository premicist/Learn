## Start with the question

When evaluating Nepal’s progress, ask whether a change increases output, expands productive opportunities, and improves people’s living conditions. These are related questions, but they are not identical.

## Growth is one part of the picture

An increase in real output or income is economic growth. It can create jobs, raise tax revenue, and support investment. The result depends on the type of growth, who receives the benefits, and whether the gains can continue over time.

## A wider development lens

Economic development also considers poverty, inequality, education, health, employment quality, access to infrastructure, and environmental sustainability. For example, a project may raise output while producing few local jobs or placing pressure on water and forests. A balanced evaluation should identify both the gain and the trade-off.

## A Nepal-focused answer structure

Begin with one relevant indicator, explain what it shows, add a distributional or employment point, and finish with a constraint or policy priority. This structure helps turn a general statement about Nepal into an economic argument.

## Practice prompt

Choose one sector such as agriculture, tourism, hydropower, or digital services. Explain one way it can support growth, one way it can support development, and one condition needed for the benefits to be broad and sustainable.
