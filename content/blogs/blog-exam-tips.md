## Start with the syllabus

Use the official syllabus as your checklist. Divide each unit into concepts, diagrams, definitions, and numerical problems, then mark the topics you can explain without looking at your notes.

## Build a one-page revision sheet

For each chapter, write the key definitions, assumptions, formulas, and diagrams on one page. Keep the wording precise: in economics, a small difference in a definition can change the meaning of an answer.

## Practise diagrams and numericals

Do not only read worked examples. Re-draw demand and supply diagrams, label every axis, and practise writing the steps for numerical questions. Show your working so that your reasoning is visible even if the final answer contains an arithmetic mistake.

## Answer according to the command word

“Define,” “explain,” “distinguish,” and “evaluate” require different kinds of answers. Begin with a direct response, add the relevant theory or diagram, and finish with an example or conclusion when the question calls for it.

## Use timed practice

Complete at least one past paper under exam conditions. Review it afterward by separating knowledge gaps from presentation problems. The next revision session should target the largest recurring weakness rather than simply repeating the chapters you already know.
