## What makes up an economy?

An economy is the system through which people, firms, and the government produce, exchange, and use goods and services. In Nepal, agriculture, tourism, remittances, trade, manufacturing, and a growing service sector all contribute to economic activity.

## Production and employment

Agriculture remains important for livelihoods and food security, while services provide employment in education, finance, transport, hospitality, communication, and public administration. Economic development therefore involves both raising farm productivity and creating productive non-farm jobs.

## The role of remittances and trade

Many Nepali households receive income from family members working abroad. These remittances support consumption, education, health, and investment, but dependence on external employment also shows why domestic opportunities and skills development matter. Nepal imports many goods and exports a narrower range of products, so the trade balance and foreign-exchange position deserve close attention.

## Key challenges

The economy faces familiar development challenges: uneven infrastructure, low productivity, limited industrial diversification, geographic barriers, and vulnerability to natural disasters and external shocks. These challenges are connected, so policy solutions require investment in human capital, reliable infrastructure, entrepreneurship, and effective institutions.

## A useful way to study the topic

When analysing Nepal’s economy, separate **growth** from **development**. Growth measures an increase in output or income; development also considers employment quality, poverty, inequality, health, education, and environmental sustainability. Keeping this distinction clear makes economic analysis more complete.
