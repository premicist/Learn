## The rational consumer is a useful starting point

Traditional consumer theory often begins with a simple assumption: people compare alternatives and choose the bundle that gives them the greatest satisfaction within their budget. This model is useful because it helps us analyse prices, income, and choice in a clear way.

## Real decisions use shortcuts

In everyday life, people have limited time, information, and attention. We therefore use mental shortcuts, or heuristics, to make decisions. These shortcuts are efficient, but they can also produce predictable patterns that differ from the fully rational model.

## Three ideas to remember

**Present bias** means that immediate rewards can receive more weight than larger future benefits. **Loss aversion** describes the tendency to feel a loss more strongly than an equivalent gain. **Anchoring** occurs when an initial number influences later judgements, even when that number is not especially informative.

## Why this matters for economics

Behavioural economics does not make traditional theory useless. Instead, it identifies when and why the standard assumptions may fail. The insights are useful for understanding saving, consumer protection, advertising, defaults, and public policy.

A good analysis can therefore use both perspectives: begin with the standard model, then ask whether limited attention, social preferences, or predictable biases change the result.
